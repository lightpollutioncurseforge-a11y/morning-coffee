A Scientist Seated at a Desk by Candlelight

Anna Dorothea Therbusch (German, 1721/22–1782) · dated 1755 · oil on canvas · 100 × 81 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art 2023.1 · CC0

Cleveland says Therbusch submitted a candlelight subject for candidacy to the Paris Academy, although such subjects were unfashionable there. She was turned down for one of the three places allotted to women “on the grounds that a woman could not have painted such an accomplished work.” She let the male committee watch her paint, and was admitted on her second attempt. Wikipedia dates her election to 1767.

A loose end: Cleveland dates it 1755, from a signature on the back. Wikipedia lists it as 1767, and says she gave up painting between her 1742 marriage and about 1760. I can’t reconcile the two.

AnecdoteParis, 23 November 1654

FEU

Three hundred seventy-two years ago tonight, Blaise Pascal wrote down two hours of his life on a sheet of paper and dated it precisely:

L’an de grâce 1654, Lundi, 23 novembre, jour de saint Clément, pape et martyr … Depuis environ dix heures et demie du soir jusques environ minuit et demi,The year of grace 1654, Monday, 23 November, feast of St. Clement, pope and martyr … From about half past ten at night until about half past midnight,

Then, in capitals: FEU. Fire. Then: “DIEU d’Abraham, DIEU d’Isaac, DIEU de Jacob, non des philosophes et des savants.” Then “Certitude. Certitude. Sentiment. Joie. Paix.” and “Joie, joie, joie, pleurs de joie.” It closes in Latin: Non obliviscar sermones tuos. Amen. “Not to forget your words.”

He didn’t publish it. He sewed it into his coat, and, in Wikipedia’s summary, “always transferred it when he changed clothes; a servant discovered it only by chance after his death” in 1662.

At 18 he had built a calculating machine to spare his father, a tax commissioner, endless recalculation. Now the first line of substance refuses the philosophers. The tidy story that a carriage accident set it off is, Wikipedia says, “disputed by some scholars.” In 2019 the Diocese of Paris said the Port-Royal chapel reads it aloud every year, on the Sunday nearest the 23rd.

Satori · Atlantic (Japan) · 1971 · Joe Yamanaka, Hideki Ishima, Jun Kozuki, George Wada

The Japanese word for a sudden awakening, on the night of Pascal’s. It’s also the deep cut from the follow-up list after Kikagaku Moyo. The benchRead from the opinions

A judge who won’t say what’s decent, and a sleeper at the border

Mercuria Energy Group v. Republic of Poland

CourtD.C. Circuit, No. 25-7152 · decided Nov. 20, 2026
HoldingAffirmed. A Swedish court’s annulment of an arbitration award against Poland stands, so U.S. courts won’t enforce the award.
Vote3–0. Randolph wrote for the court and also concurred alone. Millett and Wilkins were on the panel.

No American party is involved; the New York Convention opened U.S. courts to the claim anyway. The circuit’s test is whether a foreign annulment is “repugnant to fundamental notions of what is decent and just.” The court quoted the district judge (“It is not this Court’s place to superintend EU courts as they interpret their own laws”) and added: “Exactly so.”

The concurrence is the reason to read it. Randolph traces the test to a Restatement comment and wants out of it altogether:

Judges have no business deciding what is decent and what is moral, and they should not be required to determine whether a foreign court’s decision offends some notion of “justice.”

His footnote gives Learned Hand’s story of calling “Do justice!” after Holmes, and Holmes calling him back: “That is not my job. My job is to play the game according to the rules.”

Elizondo Urzua v. Blanche

CourtNinth Circuit, No. 25-604 · filed Nov. 17, 2026
HoldingPetition denied. Under 8 U.S.C. § 1361, the person facing removal must prove the “time, place, and manner” of entry, even outside illegal-entry cases.
Vote2–1. VanDyke wrote the opinion and Gould dissented. Nguyen was on the panel; the opinion doesn’t say separately that she joined.

The whole case turned on when he was “admitted.” He said 1994: he fell asleep in a friend’s car near the border and woke up “parked next to a taco truck in Los Angeles.” The government said 2015. The burden was his. The real fight is over precedent. A 1981 Ninth Circuit case, Iran v. INS, limited the statute to illegal entries. VanDyke calls that “broad, unreasoned dicta.” Gould answers that the Iran panel “devoted several paragraphs” to the point and that it “was essential to the decision. That reasoned decision binds us.”

Poem · SpanishGranada, 1585–86

¡Oh llama de amor viva!

San Juan de la Cruz (1542–1591) · complete, four stanzas · text from Spanish Wikisource · translation by Claude, unrhymed

Pascal wrote FEU and stopped there. John of the Cross gives the fire a whole poem, as a wound, a cautery, and lamps lit in the caverns of the senses. Wikipedia says the first version was written at Granada in 1585–86, “apparently in two weeks.” A second version followed in 1591, the year he died.

¡Oh llama de amor viva,que tiernamente hieresde mi alma en el más profundo centro!Pues ya no eres esquiva,acaba ya, si quieres;¡rompe la tela de este dulce encuentro!

O living flame of lovethat tenderly woundsmy soul in its deepest centre!Since you hold back no longer,finish now, if you will:tear the veil of this sweet meeting!

¡Oh cauterio suave!¡Oh regalada llaga!¡Oh mano blanda! ¡Oh toque delicado,que a vida eterna sabe,y toda deuda paga!Matando. Muerte en vida la has trocado.

O gentle cautery!O welcome wound!O soft hand! O delicate touchthat tastes of eternal lifeand pays every debt!Killing, you have turned death into life.

¡Oh lámparas de fuego,en cuyos resplandoreslas profundas cavernas del sentido,que estaba oscuro y ciego,con extraños primorescalor y luz dan junto a su Querido!

O lamps of fire,in whose bright shiningthe deep caverns of the senses,once dark and blind,with rare and strange skillgive heat and light together to their Beloved!

¡Cuán manso y amorosorecuerdas en mi seno,donde secretamente solo morasy en tu aspirar sabroso,de bien y gloria lleno,cuán delicadamente me enamoras!

How gently and how lovinglyyou wake within my breast,where you dwell secretly and alone,and in your sweet breathing,full of good and glory,how delicately you make me fall in love!

PaintingParis, the same year

The Kiss of Peace and Justice

Laurent de La Hyre (French, 1606–1656) · signed and dated 1654 · oil on canvas · 54.9 × 76.2 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art 1971.102 · CC0

Painted in the year of the Memorial. Justice, in blue, holds the sword and scales, and Peace burns the weapons of war. The stone reads IVSTITIA ET PAX OSCULATAE SUNT and cites “Psal. 84.” Cleveland gives the verse as Psalm 85:10. Its political reading is that 1654 is the end of the Fronde, when the parlements and the nobles tried and failed to limit the crown. If Cleveland’s reading holds, this is peace on the crown’s terms. Set it beside Randolph, who would rather not be asked what justice is.

PhotographAuvergne, c. 1890–1900

Summit of the Puy de Dôme

Detroit Publishing Co. photochrom, print no. 9842 · between c. 1890 and c. 1900 · Library of Congress LC-DIG-ppmsc-05004 · no known restrictions

The ruins are a temple of Mercury, and the print’s own caption names the building on the crest as an observatory. Pascal was too ill to climb up here himself. On 19 September 1648 his brother-in-law Florin Périer carried a tube of mercury to the top and found it stood at “only 23" and 2 lines.” He repeated it “five times with care.” That was six years before the fire.

Mosque Lamp

Turkey, Iznik, Ottoman period · 1585–95 · fritware with underglaze design · 28.4 × 19.5 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art 1944.236 · CC0

It was made in the same years as the poem’s “lámparas de fuego,” and it is too opaque to have been much use for light. Cleveland thinks it hung on chains for beauty, from those turquoise loops, and possibly as an acoustic device “softening the echo of prayer.” The body is fritware: ground quartz and glass frit with a little white clay, made to approximate Chinese porcelain.

SongDüsseldorf, 1981

Kraftwerk, “Pocket Calculator”

Computer World · Kling Klang / EMI · single 21 April 1981, album 11 May 1981 · also as “Taschenrechner,” “Mini Calculateur” and “Dentaku”

For the machine Pascal built before the fire. His own verdict on it is fragment 340 of the Pensées:

The arithmetical machine produces effects which approach nearer to thought than all the actions of animals. But it does nothing which would enable us to attribute will to it, as to the animals.Pascal, Pensées 340, tr. W. F. Trotter

Kraftwerk issued the song in English, German, French and Japanese. The US single came on lime vinyl to match the cover. Not everyone was charmed. Smash Hits gave the album 4 out of 10 and complained the band were content “to tootle around on their instrument panels in the service of a bunch of non-songs about pocket calculators and computers.”

AIWho sits in the judge’s chair

An Indian Supreme Court justice proposes 24/7 AI divorce courts

At a conclave in Jabalpur on Saturday, Justice Joymalya Bagchi said India is “sitting on a pile of over five crore cases,” which is 50 million. He proposed taking mutual-consent divorces and early-hearing requests away from judges: “This should be completely taken away from the human agency.” He would keep constitutional cases for human judges. On the same panel, Senior Advocate Rajshekhar Rao replied: “access to a chatbot is not access to justice.”

An English family judge discounts an AI-written expert report

This was reported on Saturday; the judgment is dated 30 September. In Cornwall Council v M, an independent social worker admitted that Microsoft Copilot had generated a whole kinship assessment from her notes. HHJ Flexman accepted “a sliding scale of use.” Transcription and spelling are fine, but generating the report “strays into the latter.” Copilot’s own summary had been left in the text. Under the expert rules, she wrote, AI “could be akin to third party involvement.” She gave the evidence little weight, faulting its rigour as well.
