A tiger crouches under two columns of calligraphy. The signature at the bottom names a famous painter and the year 1772. Who wrote the words, and does the museum believe the signature?

Tiger in Wind

Attributed by its signature to Maruyama Ōkyo (Japanese, 1733–1795); the museum dates it to the 1800s · hanging scroll, ink on paper · 134.6 × 58 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art 1971.232 · CC0

The seals beside the inscription suggest it was brushed by Yūjō (1723–1773), an imperial prince who became a Buddhist monk and abbot of Enman’in at Miidera. The line is from a poem attributed to Yu Ruyu, a Yuan-dynasty poet whose dates are unknown. In the museum’s words, it describes “the sound of a fierce wind lashing the ground. Wind is associated with the roar of the tiger.”

Yūjō was Ōkyo’s most important patron when the painter was young. The signature says Ōkyo made this in 1772, the year before Yūjō died. Cleveland doesn’t believe it: the work “would appear to be one imagining their relationship, as opposed to a genuine piece.” So someone, later, painted a friendship. Here the provenance twist is the whole picture. Like your bronze masks, it stares back.

Song · 2:49One voice, no band

Dead Can Dance, “The Wind That Shakes the Barley”

Into the Labyrinth · 4AD · 1993 · Lisa Gerrard, voice · words and music by Robert Dwyer Joyce, arranged by Dead Can Dance

The ballad’s speaker is a young Wexford rebel of 1798 who leaves his love for the fighting. The barley comes from the rebels’ habit of carrying grain in their pockets as rations on the march. Robert Dwyer Joyce, a Limerick poet and professor of English, wrote it. Wikipedia’s album page says this track is sung a cappella, though that section is uncited. Gerrard on why she recorded it: “it was meant to be a rallying song, but it has such an intense sadness that it becomes an anti-war song.”

It’s on the 4AD label, like the Cocteau Twins in your likes, and it sits near your murder ballads. The album’s title, and tracks called “Ariadne” and “Towards the Within,” point at Theseus and the labyrinth.

Anecdote and photographTacoma, 7 November 1940

Galloping Gertie

The main span falling, 7 November 1940 · Wide World photo · Library of Congress LC-USZ62-46682 · no known restrictions; no copyright renewal found

The state engineer, Clark Eldridge, wanted 25-foot trusses under the roadway to stiffen it. Leon Moisseiff, a consultant on the Golden Gate, had a newer theory and offered slim eight-foot plate girders instead. That design was more elegant and cheaper, so it won. While the deck was going up, workers noticed it moving in the wind and called it “Galloping Gertie.” It opened on 1 July 1940. Farquharson, a University of Washington professor, tested scale models and on 2 November recommended adding fairings. Five days later, in winds of about 40 miles an hour, the deck began to twist.

Leonard Coatsworth, an editor at the News Tribune, was on the bridge in his car with his daughter’s three-legged cocker spaniel, Tubby:

Around me I could hear concrete cracking. I started back to the car to get the dog, but was thrown before I could reach it.Leonard Coatsworth, as quoted on Wikipedia

He crawled about 500 yards on hands and knees to the tower and saw his car go into the Narrows. The collapse killed Tubby. The state later paid Coatsworth $814.40 for the car and its contents, including the dog. Many physics textbooks present it as simple resonance. Wikipedia says it was really aeroelastic flutter, which feeds on itself: above about 35 mph, every twist made the next one larger.

Listen alongsideSame state, six months later

Woody Guthrie, “Roll On, Columbia”

Columbia River Collection · Rounder · released 1987 · Wikipedia gives 1941 as the recording year · tune adapted from Huddie Ledbetter’s “Goodnight, Irene”

In May 1941 the federal government hired Guthrie for one month to write songs about the Columbia River dams. He wrote 26. They were propaganda, commissioned at Alan Lomax’s suggestion to win support for federal power. This one became Washington’s official folk song in 1987. Its last verses praise Grand Coulee as “the mightiest thing ever built by a man.” It’s from your Almanac Singers end, and it comes from the same state, months after Tacoma’s span fell.

Poem · RussianMunich, October 1830

Autumn Evening

Fyodor Tyutchev (1803–1873) · complete, 12 lines · text from the 1987 Biblioteka poeta edition, via Russian Wikisource · translation by Claude, unrhymed

Tyutchev was a Russian diplomat in Munich for 22 years. He knew Heine and Schelling there and thought his own poems were trifles. This one first appeared in 1840, signed only “F. T---v.” His first book came out in 1854, put together by Turgenev and others without his help. You discount verse heard in translation, so the Russian comes first. Watch the turn at line 9: the whole autumn becomes one face.

Есть в светлости осенних вечеровУмильная, таинственная прелесть!..Зловещий блеск и пестрота дерёв,Багряных листьев томный, легкий шелест,

There is in the brightness of autumn eveningsa tender and mysterious charm!..The ominous glitter and motley of the trees,the languid, light rustle of crimson leaves,

Туманная и тихая лазурьНад грустно-сиротеющей землеюИ, как предчувствие сходящих бурь,Порывистый, холодный ветр порою,

the misty and quiet azureabove the sadly orphaning earth,and, like a foreboding of storms coming down,at times a gusting, cold wind:

Ущерб, изнеможенье — и на всемТа кроткая улыбка увяданья,Что в существе разумном мы зовемБожественной стыдливостью страданья!

waning, exhaustion, and on everythingthat meek smile of witheringwhich, in a rational being, we callthe divine modesty of suffering!

The poem is one stanza. It’s split into fours here to fit a phone.

A sanctuary memo, and what AI does to a fee bill

Mennonite Church USA v. Department of Homeland Security

CourtD.C. Circuit, No. 25-5209 · argued Feb. 5, decided Oct. 20, 2026
HoldingReversed. The 27 plaintiffs likely have associational standing to seek a preliminary injunction, so the denial is reversed and the case is sent back for further consideration of that motion. The merits aren’t decided.
Vote2–1. Pan wrote for the court, joined by Wilkins. Rao dissented.
BelowD.D.C. No. 1:25-cv-00403 denied the injunction for likely lack of standing. I didn’t check which district judge.

For more than thirty years, in Pan’s words, the government “largely refrained” from immigration enforcement at “sensitive locations,” including houses of worship. DHS changed that on 20 January 2025. Twenty-seven Christian and Jewish organizations sued under the First Amendment, RFRA and the APA. They asked for the old policy back and for a bar on enforcement at worship sites without a judicial warrant or exigent circumstances. The majority found standing on one theory only, citing “the cardinal principle of judicial restraint,” which counsels that “if it is not necessary to decide more, it is necessary not to decide more.” Rao’s dissent goes to who governs enforcement:

Enforcement priorities and policies are the exclusive purview of the Executive Branch, and supervising these choices is beyond the judicial province.Rao, J., dissenting

Her other point is that the old memo “imposed no actual restriction on enforcement authority or discretion,” so restoring it would change nothing. That’s your political-theology shelf, as a live case: a church’s claim on space against the executive’s discretion.

Flux Drive Inc. v. Rong

Court10th Circuit, Nos. 25-1308, 26-1013 & 26-1292 · filed Oct. 30, 2026 · published
HoldingAffirmed. No personal jurisdiction over the Illinois defendants in a Colorado trade-secrets suit, and $114,663.11 in fees plus supplemental fees stands.
VoteUnanimous. McHugh wrote for Matheson, McHugh and Federico. No separate opinions.

Yesterday I said I couldn’t read this one. Today I could. The AI point is a single paragraph. The losing plaintiffs argued that because defense counsel used AI, they had billed too many hours. The court allowed that AI may “reduce[] the number of hours required,” but:

As the magistrate judge explained, however, ethical rules require counsel to verify the cases cited and to ensure that all arguments are grounded in legal authority.McHugh, J., for the panel

So the plaintiffs “have not shown that defense counsel’s use of AI rendered the hours that they actually expended unreasonable.” The machine’s output doesn’t count until a lawyer has checked it, and the checking is billable.

Artifact21 cm tall, tongue out

Painted Gorgon antefix

Greek, South Italian, Tarentine · Archaic · ca. 540 BCE · terracotta, paint · 21 × 26.2 cm · Metropolitan Museum 39.11.9 · public domain

An antefix is the block at the eaves that hides the end of a row of roof tiles. On a temple it might carry a satyr or a maenad. The Gorgon’s face, with its petrifying eyes and teeth, was a popular choice because it was thought to ward off evil. Archaic Gorgons were always made ugly; only later did artists make them beautiful. The name may be the oldest sound effect in Greek: one scholar, Thalia Feldman, suggests the root is the “grrr of a growling beast.” Here is your fierce face again, and today’s tiger roars too.

PaintingProuts Neck, Maine

Early Morning After a Storm at Sea

Winslow Homer (American, 1836–1910) · 1900–1903 · oil on canvas · 76.8 × 127 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art 1924.195 · CC0

The wind has dropped and the sea hasn’t yet. Homer called this “the best picture of the sea that I have painted.” It began as a watercolour. Nearly twenty years later he took it up again in oil and waited for the right weather, painting it in four sessions over two years. It’s the opposite of Tacoma: not a structure caught in the wind, but a painter who waited for it.

AIWho checks the machine

Pennsylvania may make filers certify they checked the AI

A committee that advises the Pennsylvania Supreme Court has proposed that anyone filing AI-generated content, lawyers and self-represented people alike, must “thoroughly review” its accuracy and certify that they did. That covers summaries of legal principles as well as every citation and quote. Sanctions could run from a reprimand to fines to dismissal of a case. Spotlight PA’s summary: “The proposal would effectively place the accuracy burden on humans, rather than technology.” Comments close on 24 November, and then the seven justices vote. It’s the same rule the Tenth Circuit leaned on above. I read Spotlight PA’s report, not the notice in the Pennsylvania Bulletin.

An EU court adviser on chatbots that reuse the news

On 3 November Advocate General Maciej Szpunar gave his opinion in Like Company (C-250/25), described as the first generative-AI case before the EU Court of Justice. A Hungarian web publisher is suing Google Ireland. He would throw out the questions about training, because there’s no evidence the articles were used and training isn’t an “online use” of press publications. Answers are different. A chatbot can infringe if it got the text unlawfully, for instance around a paywall, or if it reuses the article’s form of expression recognisably. The provider is liable where such uses are “inherent to its business model.” It isn’t binding. The Court’s site refused me, so this is from a law blog’s summary.
