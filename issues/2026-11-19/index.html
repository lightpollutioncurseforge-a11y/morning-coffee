AnecdoteVienna, November 1828

Or something else

Schubert died 198 years ago today, at three in the afternoon, in his brother Ferdinand’s flat. About a week earlier, on or around 11 November, he wrote what became his last letter, to his old friend Franz von Schober. It is undated; Schober noted that he received it on the 12th. In Richard Law’s translation:

I am ill. For the last 11 days I have not eaten or drunk anything and shift exhausted and staggering from chair to bed and back. … Of [James Fenimore] Cooper’s books I have read The Last Mohican, The Spy, The Pilot, The Pioneers. If you have something else by him, I pray you to deposit it with Franz von Bogner in the coffee house. My brother, conscientiousness itself, will bring me the same conscientiously. Or something else.Schubert to Schober, Deutsch, Dok 546

Law reads the coffee-house drop as sparing Schober any risk of infection. As far as anyone knows, Schober never came, never wrote, and sent no books. The friend who did come, Eduard Bauernfeld, left the shortest account: “On Monday I still spoke with him. On Tuesday he was delirious. On Wednesday he was dead.”

Law has no patience for legend. A bedside string quartet in the last week, which Wikipedia still tells, is “probably nonsense.” Those who hear Winterreise as premonition “need to get a grip.” It was typhoid, and the death “unfortunate, but not tragic or predestined.”

Listen alongsideThe last song

Gerald Finley & Julius Drake, “Die Taubenpost”

Schubert, D 965a, words by Johann Gabriel Seidl · on Schwanengesang · Hyperion CDA68288 · recorded 2018, St Silas the Martyr, London

“The Pigeon Post” is usually counted Schubert’s last song, from 1828. His publisher Tobias Haslinger tacked it onto the posthumous Schwanengesang, and Hyperion lists it as a separate track. In the last stanza the pigeon’s name turns out to be Sehnsucht, longing. MusicWeb’s John Quinn hears this pair take it slower than usual, “tinged with melancholy,” so that “the longing is unfulfilled.” Your classical likes run to single, direct pieces like Dido’s Lament; this is four minutes.

Circular altar with Hermes and Pallas Athena

Roman, said to be from Corfu · 100 BCE–100 CE · marble · 80 × 53.3 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art 1915.559 · CC0

The messenger god, or maybe not. This is about a sixth of a round altar or statue base whose full ring held twelve figures, probably the Olympians. Athena is certain: spear, helmet, aegis. Her companion is Hermes with his staff or Hephaistos with his hammer, and Cleveland won’t choose. The carver put Archaic zigzag folds on taller, later bodies. Corfu is the island of the Lear drawings you saved.

Who speaks for the United States

United States ex rel. Jackson v. Ventavia Research Group

CourtFifth Circuit, No. 24-40564 · Nov. 13, 2026
HoldingAffirmed. The government could intervene late, for “good cause,” and dismiss a relator’s False Claims Act suit; her retaliation claim also fails.
Vote3–0. Willett for Elrod, C.J., Richman and himself. Willett also concurs; Elrod concurs.

Brook Jackson sued on the government’s behalf, alleging problems in Pfizer’s COVID-19 vaccine trials at a site Ventavia ran. The government declined to join, then stepped in to end it. The majority’s rule is short: “A relator may bring the suit, but the Government retains the right to seek to end it.”

Then Willett, writing alone, asks whether private relators can exist at all under Article II, through the Appointments, Vesting and Take Care Clauses:

Who speaks for the United States in a lawsuit brought in its name? Under the False Claims Act, not always the United States.
The Appointments Clause lists three ways to make an inferior officer. Self-appointment by filing a sealed complaint is not one of them.

Nobody raised the question below or on appeal, and the circuit’s 2001 en banc decision in Riley binds the panel. Willett: “Riley binds us today. Whether it binds us tomorrow is for the en banc court.” The Chief Judge calls the concerns “no doubt en banc worthy.” It’s your question about machines and authority, asked of people.

American Alliance for Equal Rights v. Illinois

CourtSeventh Circuit, Nos. 25-2461 & 25-2487 · Nov. 18, 2026
HoldingReversed and remanded. The Alliance and the United States both have standing to challenge Illinois’s S.B. 2930. The merits are left to the district court.
Vote3–0. Kirsch for Hamilton, Maldonado and himself. No separate opinions.

The 2024 law makes certain nonprofits collect demographic data from their directors and officers and post it on their websites. The Alliance calls that compelled speech and induced discrimination. The district court found standing lacking. The panel held that the United States may sue to enforce equal protection, that it’s a fair inference some officer will answer, and that members may stay pseudonymous. Two lines:

The federal government may bring a suit to enforce its laws even when a private individual cannot.
When the law tells corporate officers and directors to do something, they typically comply.

Beauty Reading a Letter

Tōensai Kanshi (Japanese, active c. 1751–72) · hanging scroll, ink and color on paper · Cleveland Museum of Art 2026.45, given this year · CC0

Two readers, one letter. The woman has slipped her right arm out of her sleeve, and Cleveland reads her hand as pressed to her pulse, “as if to still her excitement.” Behind the paper door a second woman watches through a tear: kaimami, Cleveland notes, covert viewing, often with romantic intent. Kanshi is barely known: a pencilled tag in the box says only that he was “Known for a few fine painting of bijin-ga.”

SongLuk thung, Thailand, 1968

Suraphol Sombatcharoen, “Siphok Pi Haeng Khwam Lang”

“16 Years of Our Past” · released shortly before his death in August 1968 · Wikipedia lists an album titled 16 Years The Past, which I couldn’t date

Another last song. Suraphol was called the “King of Luk Thung,” and his first hit came in 1954. As a navy nursing student he kept slipping out to sing at night, was jailed for it, and sang to the other inmates before sleep. This, his best-known song, is about the end of his own 16-year marriage, “both the happiness and bitterness of the union.” Shortly after, he was shot dead after a show in Nakhon Pathom; the motive is unknown. You’ve liked Thai singers from Sound of Siam; this is the genre’s king.

Poem · Old NorseIceland, from the Poetic Edda

Hávamál, stanzas 70–77

Anonymous · text as edited by Guðni Jónsson, from heimskringla.no · six stanzas, with 73 and 75 cut · translation by Claude, unrhymed

Grillparzer’s line on Schubert’s grave is a verdict: “The art of music buried here a rich possession, but still many more beautiful hopes.” Hávamál, the Eddic “Sayings of the High One,” knew the genre: be alive, leave kin to raise the stone, and know that what never dies is the judgment on the dead. Stanza 74 has an autumn night in it.

Betra er lifðumen sé ólifðum,ey getr kvikr kú;eld sá ek upp brennaauðgum manni fyrir,en úti var dauðr fyr durum.

Better to be livingthan not living;the live man always gets the cow.I saw the fire blaze upfor the rich man,and he lay dead outside the door.

Haltr ríðr hrossi,hjörð rekr handar vanr,daufr vegr ok dugir,blindr er betrien brenndr séi,nýtr manngi nás.

The lame man rides,the handless man drives the herd,the deaf man fights and does well;better be blindthan be burned:no one has use for a corpse.

Sonr er betri,þótt sé síð of alinneftir genginn guma;sjaldan bautarsteinarstanda brautu nær,nema reisi niðr at nið.

A son is better,though born late,after the man is gone;seldom do memorial stonesstand near the roadunless kin raise them for kin.

Artifact19 cm tall

Eucharistic dove

Limoges, France · ca. 1215–35 · gilded copper with champlevé enamel · 19 × 19.8 cm · Metropolitan Museum 17.190.344 · public domain

Another pigeon carrying something. In medieval Western churches, doves like this kept the consecrated host, in a pyx slipped in through a flap in the bird’s back. Many were Limoges work, mostly gilt, with enamel on the wings and eyes. J. Pierpont Morgan gave this one to the Met in 1917. Gilt against enamel blue is your orange-and-blue palette, early.

AIMachines and authority

New York bill: a judge’s fake citations would be misconduct

Senate bill S10741, from Sen. Michelle Hinchey, was introduced on 16 November and sent to the Rules Committee. It would make it misconduct for a judge to knowingly or recklessly include, rely on or adopt “material fabricated legal authority” in a ruling. The Commission on Judicial Conduct could sanction the judge or refer the finding to the Assembly Speaker to consider impeachment. The key line puts the duty on the judge, not the tool: “The use of artificial intelligence, standing alone, shall not constitute misconduct.”

U.S. Catholic bishops form an AI task force

At the bishops’ fall meeting in Baltimore, Bishop Oscar Cantú of San Jose, who chairs the new task force on AI and human dignity, said it will lobby for “clear guardrails on the development and use of AI.” Their updated investment guidelines say the conference will not invest in companies whose AI technologies “fail to safeguard the dignity of the human person.” Cantú: “The Church possesses something that Silicon Valley cannot engineer.” The conference president, Archbishop Paul Coakley, called AI “a faint reflection of human intelligence.” A report that the new guidelines also exclude autonomous weapons I couldn’t open, so I’ve left it out.
