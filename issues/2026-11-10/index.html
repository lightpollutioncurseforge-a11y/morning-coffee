Twilight in the Wilderness

Frederic Edwin Church (American, 1826–1900) · 1860 · oil on canvas · 101.6 × 162.6 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art 1965.233 · CC0

Maine, near Mount Katahdin, painted in Church’s New York studio from a sketch made nearly two years earlier. Instead of debuting it at an annual exhibition with other artists, as was the custom, Church showed it alone at a prestigious gallery; several hundred people came during its seven-week run.

Cleveland notes the date, on the eve of the Civil War, and says the subject “can be interpreted as symbolically evoking the coming conflagration.” It also has a cameo in Clear and Present Danger. Orange against blue is the most common palette in your saved images. This is that palette at full volume.

Song · 2:54Rio de Janeiro, 1972

Arthur Verocai, “Na Boca do Sol”

Arthur Verocai · Continental SLP-10.079 · 1972 · written by Victor Martins and Arthur Verocai

Near Hareton Salvanini in your likes, with a provenance story. Verocai studied civil engineering, and was producing albums for Elis Regina, Jorge Ben and Ivan Lins when he made his own, which he calls “samba mixed with soul.” It was largely ignored and sold very few copies. He stopped composing for 30 years and arranged advertising jingles instead.

Then hip-hop found it. MF Doom sampled this track for “Orris Root Powder” in 2005, and Madlib said, “I could listen to this album everyday for the rest of my life.” Discogs puts the median price of an original LP at $2,594. I haven’t heard it.

Artifact35 cm tall

Conch shell with a figure of Hevajra

Cambodia, Khmer, Angkor period · 1100s · shell and bronze · Cleveland Museum of Art 1977.176 · CC0

An instrument and a sacred image at once. Cleveland says conches were blown at the start of rituals in royal temples like Banteay Chhmar, to make the sound of the syllable om. Worked into the bronze is Hevajra, dancing, with eight heads, sixteen arms and four legs. The three-serpent stand is probably not original.

Your likes run to Cambodian pop of the 1960s; this is Cambodia some eight centuries earlier. The provenance names only Spink & Son, London, the seller, with no date or find-spot.

AnecdoteLake Superior, 10 November 1975

We are holding our own

Fifty-one years ago tonight, the Edmund Fitzgerald, the largest ship ever lost on the Great Lakes, was carrying 26,116 long tons of taconite pellets from Superior, Wisconsin, to a steel mill near Detroit. The forecast was ordinary for November. One captain, Dudley Paquette of the Wilfred Sykes, read the weather differently and kept to the shelter of the north shore.

By late afternoon the Fitzgerald had lost two vent covers, then both radars, and was steering by the Arthur M. Anderson’s. The beacon at Whitefish Point was out. Captain Ernest McSorley told another ship: “I have a ‘bad list’, I have lost both radars, and am taking heavy seas over the deck in one of the worst seas I have ever been in.” At about 7:10 the Anderson asked how she was doing.

We are holding our own.

Ten minutes later she was gone from radio and radar, with all 29 men and no distress call. When the Anderson reached the Coast Guard, the officer asked it to watch for a lost 16-foot boat. A petty officer later testified: “I considered it serious, but at the time it was not urgent.”

Why she sank is still disputed. The Coast Guard blamed hatch covers not fully clamped, letting the hold flood slowly. The Lake Carriers’ Association objected. The NTSB found the hatch covers had collapsed under “giant boarding seas.”

Listen alongsideA ballad that took a correction

Gordon Lightfoot, “The Wreck of the Edmund Fitzgerald”

Summertime Dream · 1976

Your narrative ballads, about the news. Lightfoot said he wrote it after Newsweek misspelled the ship “Edmond” two weeks after the sinking, which he felt dishonored the 29. His original line named a cause: “At 7 p.m. a main hatchway caved in.” In light of new evidence he changed it on stage to “At 7 p.m. it grew dark, it was then.” When he died in May 2023, Detroit’s Mariners’ Church tolled its bell 30 times: 29 for the crew, one for him.

Poem · VietnameseVietnam, before 1820

The Tale of Kiều, the opening

Nguyễn Du (1765–1820) · Truyện Kiều, lines 1–8 and 29–34 of 3,254 · Vietnamese text from Wikisource · translation by Claude, unrhymed

It was on your October reading list. Nguyễn Du took the plot of a 17th-century Chinese romance and made it, in six-eight verse, the life of a gifted young woman who sells herself into marriage to get her father and brother out of prison. The man turns out to be a pimp. The poem opens by setting talent against fate.

Trăm năm trong cõi người ta,Chữ tài chữ mệnh khéo là ghét nhau.Trải qua một cuộc bể dâu,Những điều trông thấy mà đau đớn lòng.

A hundred years in this world of ours:the word “talent” and the word “fate” are clever at hating each other.Live through one turn of sea into mulberry field,and what you see there wounds the heart.

Lạ gì bỉ sắc tư phong,Trời xanh quen thói má hồng đánh ghen.Cảo thơm lần giở trước đèn,Phong tình cổ lục còn truyền sử xanh.

No wonder: what is stinted here is lavished there,and blue heaven, out of habit, strikes at rosy cheeks in envy.Turning fragrant pages slowly by the lamp:an old record of love, still handed down in the green annals.

· · · twenty lines cut: the Ming, the Vương family, two daughters and their beauty · · ·

Thông minh vốn sẵn tư trời,Pha nghề thi họa, đủ mùi ca ngâm.Cung thương làu bậc ngũ âm,Nghề riêng ăn đứt Hồ cầm một trương.Khúc nhà tay lựa nên chương,Một thiên bạc mệnh, lại càng não nhân.

Quick of mind, a gift from heaven,she had poetry and painting, every flavor of song and chant.She knew the modes and the five notes by heart,and on the Hu lute no one came near her.Her own hand chose the notes and made a piece:one song, “Thin Fate,” that grieved people all the more.

What kind of claim, and whose speech

Mullin v. Friends of the Ruidosa Church

CourtU.S. Supreme Court, No. 26A560 · Nov. 9, 2026 · per curiam
HoldingStay granted of a Western District of Texas injunction that halted border construction in the Big Bend Sector. A claim that the Secretary exceeded his statutory authority isn’t constitutional, whatever it’s called.
VoteUnsigned. Jackson would deny, without an opinion. Others not stated.
BelowW.D. Tex. No. 3:26-cv-01099. The opinion doesn’t name the judge. The Fifth Circuit has no jurisdiction under the statute.

A 1996 statute, as amended, lets the Secretary of Homeland Security waive “all legal requirements” to build border barriers quickly, and lets courts hear only constitutional claims. The Secretary waived environmental and conservation laws along the Big Bend. A river guide, an environmental nonprofit and a group preserving the historic church at Ruidosa, Texas, sued under “the major questions doctrine under the U. S. Constitution, Article I, Section 1.” The district court accepted that framing. The Court did not:

There is no such thing as a “constitutional” major-questions claim.

The government said the injunction cost it nearly half a million dollars a day that “cannot be recouped.” The Court added that Congress had already preferred quick construction over “the very harms respondents assert.”

U.S. Chamber of Commerce v. Sanchez

CourtNinth Circuit, No. 25-5327 · Nov. 9, 2026 · published
HoldingCalifornia’s SB 253 Scope 1 and 2 emissions reporting likely survives; its Scope 3 reporting and all of SB 261 likely violate the First Amendment. Preliminary injunction reversed as to those; severability remanded.
VoteNguyen wrote, joined by District Judge Matsumoto (E.D.N.Y., by designation). Bennett concurred in the judgment in part and dissented in part.

Emissions from a company’s own operations are factual and bear on capital transactions, so they’re commercial speech; in the majority’s phrase, “emissions disclosures function like product warnings in capital markets.” Scope 3, the emissions of suppliers and customers, is “insufficiently tethered to the reporting entity’s own operations,” and SB 261 compels “subjective ideological assessments.” Both fail strict scrutiny.

Bennett would enjoin both laws. If closing information gaps for investors is enough, he asks, could government make workers publish “prior income, work attendance records, and reasons for past leave”? ESG Today reports that the first Scope 1 and 2 reports are due today.

Artifact26 cm tall

Warrior figurine with removable headdress

Maya · Jaina Island region, Campeche, Mexico · 600–900 · earthenware with pigment · Cleveland Museum of Art 1963.93 · CC0

A grave offering. Cleveland says the major offerings in the Maya graves of Jaina Island were ceramic figurines, “some of Mesoamerica’s finest.” This warrior probably once held weapons, and wears quilted cotton armor or the feathered uniform of a military order. The headdress lifts off. The museum is candid: “We don’t know why such figures are often pot-bellied.”

Your saved objects run to armed and fierce figures. This one is both, and a little comic.

AIWho answers for the words

Copilot left its own note in an expert’s report

In care proceedings in Truro, an independent social worker’s kinship assessment contained this: “This version is about half the length, avoids advocacy, acknowledges the children's complexities, and is more likely to withstand cross-examination.” In evidence she said the whole assessment was AI-generated from her notes, because it “produces better English than I use myself.” The judge saw no significant issue with help on transcription or grammar. But generating the whole report “has placed a significant gap between the voice and opinion of the expert and the report which she has filed.” The judge placed no significant weight on her evidence, also finding it “lacking in rigour and clarity of thought.”

Swiss churches: technology does not write history

Five Swiss church bodies, Protestant, Catholic, Old Catholic and evangelical, issued ten joint guidelines on AI in October, titled “Humanity in the Digital Age.” Among the theses: people differ from machines, and technology does not write history. In Christian Daily’s summary, a named individual must always remain responsible. I read that report, not the document.
