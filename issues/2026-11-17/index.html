AnecdoteWhitby, 17 November 680

The bell at Hackness

Bede gives the date to the day. Hilda, abbess of Streaneshalch, which his translator glosses as Whitby, died “on the 17th of November” in 680, aged sixty-six, after six years of fever.

That night, thirteen miles away at Hackness, a house Hilda had founded that same year, a nun named Begu was asleep in the dormitory. She

on a sudden heard the well known sound of a bell in the air, which used to awake and call them to prayers, when any one of them was taken out of this worldBede, Ecclesiastical History IV.23

She saw the roof open and Hilda’s soul carried up by angels. The sisters prayed and sang psalms until morning. At daybreak brothers came from Whitby with the news. “They answered that they knew it before.”

Bede’s next chapter is about a man in Hilda’s house who couldn’t sing. At feasts, “when he saw the instrument come towards him, he rose up from table and returned home.” One night, asleep in the stable, he heard: “Caedmon, sing some song to me.” “I cannot sing.” “However, you shall sing.” “What shall I sing?” “Sing the beginning of created beings.” He did. Hilda judged it grace and made him a monk.

Bede gives the hymn only in paraphrase, warning that verses “cannot be literally translated out of one language into another, without losing much of their beauty and loftiness.”

ArtifactHandle 36.9 cm

Figurative harp (domu or kundi)

Mangbetu-style maker · Democratic Republic of the Congo · by 1928 · wood, rawhide, plant fiber, metal, glass bead · Cleveland Museum of Art 1929.344 · CC0

A harp for accompanying song. Five-stringed harps with human decoration are found among several peoples in northern Congo, but “the elongated skull of this work is characteristic of the Mangbetu.” Such harps “commonly served to accompany songs by either male or female musicians,” plucked with the fingers. The painter Paul Travis acquired it at Ekibondo in 1928 for the African Art Sponsors and the Gilpin Players. The little head at the top of the neck looks back down the strings at whoever is playing.

SongAll Souls, 1965

The Watersons, “Souling Song”

Frost and Fire: A Calendar of Ritual and Magic Songs · Topic 12T136 · 1965 · Lal, Mike and Norma Waterson with John Harrison · recorded by Bill Leader in his Camden Town flat · 1:26

For a Steeleye Span and Shirley Collins listener, an earlier, barer revival record. Souling was a door-to-door custom around All Souls, 2 November. A. L. Lloyd’s sleeve note says children begged soul cakes as “food for the momentarily-returning dead,” and calls the tune, “based simply on a scale of three adjacent notes within a minor third,” “one of the most primitive we have.”

The rest of Lloyd’s note is Frazer for the folk club (“Man was on the point of inventing the gods”), a progress story to argue with. One coincidence: the 1961 LP with the Antrobus mummers’ “Soul Cakers” came out on a label called Caedmon. I haven’t heard the track.

Who is speaking, and what can wait

Khatibi v. Yip

CourtSupreme Court, No. 25-1240 · Nov. 16, 2026 · dissent from denial of certiorari
HoldingReview denied. That leaves standing the Ninth Circuit’s ruling that California’s requirement that continuing medical education courses discuss implicit bias is government speech, so the First Amendment doesn’t reach it.
VoteDenied. Thomas dissented, joined by Alito. Gorsuch would have granted, without writing.

Thomas says the Ninth Circuit collapsed the three Shurtleff factors (history, public perception, government control) into one: how much the state regulates. Then further: “Shurtleff is leading courts astray,” and the Court should reconsider it.

It cannot be the case that compelled speech becomes government speech because the government has compelled the speech.

His shortest version: “A State cannot regulate its way out of First Amendment scrutiny.”

Holy Trinity Ukrainian Catholic Church v. Township of Collier

CourtThird Circuit, No. 26-2072 · panel opinion Oct. 19; rehearing en banc denied Nov. 16, 2026 · precedential
HoldingA Ukrainian Catholic parish outside Pittsburgh is likely to win its claim under the federal religious land-use statute, RLUIPA, after the township refused to rezone for its shrine. It still gets no preliminary injunction, because its harms can be repaired later.
VotePanel 2–1: Bibas wrote, with Montgomery-Reeves; Matey dissented. En banc denied: Matey, Phipps, Bove and Mascott would have reheard it, and Phipps wrote for the four.

The parish planned a shrine to Mary with a pro-life message: a 13,000-square-foot chapel “with gold domes, a bell tower,” a museum and a retreat center. Bibas opens: “Preliminary injunctions exist to preserve cases, not decide them.” On the parish’s lost fundraising: “Lost time for worshipping is irreparable; lost time for fundraising can be compensated with money damages.” Phipps, for the four:

The Opinion in this case could give the misimpression that our Nation was founded on a commitment to municipal governance instead of a dedication to religious freedom.

His close: “By favoring local regulation over the protection of federal rights, the Opinion keeps unfortunate company, and by leaving that error uncorrected, this Court does too.”

PhotographPaper negative, c. 1855

Whitby

Benjamin Brecknell Turner (English, 1815–1894) · c. 1855 · albumen print · 28.4 × 39 cm · Art Institute of Chicago 100306 · public domain

Turner, “the scion of a successful manufacturing family,” took up photography as a hobby in 1849. He was drawn, the Art Institute says, to “the ancient Whitby Abbey,” and published six views of it. His paper negatives tended to burn out the sky; “here, he solved the problem by presenting the sky as interesting shapes framed by the bones of the church.” The museum doesn’t say how much of what he photographed goes back to Hilda’s day, and I couldn’t check.

Poem · GalicianFollas novas, 1880

Cando penso que te fuches

Rosalía de Castro · from Follas novas (1880), part II, “¡Do íntimo!”, no. XX · known as “Negra sombra” · complete · translation by Claude

Galician, not Spanish. An English translator of Rosalía thinks it was probably written after two of her babies died within months of each other, in 1876 and 1877. The hinge word won’t cross into English: asombrar means both to cast shade and to haunt, and Rosalía sets the shadow, sombra, against it twice.

Cando penso que te fuches,negra sombra que me asombras,ó pé dos meus cabezalestornas facéndome mofa.

When I think that you have gone,black shadow that haunts me,there at the foot of my pillowsyou come back, mocking me.

Cando maxino que es ida,no mesmo sol te me amostras,i eres a estrela que brila,i eres o vento que zoa.

When I imagine you’ve left,you show yourself in the sun itself,and you are the star that shines,and you are the wind that hums.

Si cantan, es ti que cantas,si choran, es ti que choras,i es o marmurio do ríoi es a noite i es a aurora.

If they sing, it is you who sing,if they weep, it is you who weep,and you’re the murmur of the river,and you’re the night and the dawn.

En todo estás e ti es todo,pra min i en min mesma moras,nin me abandonarás nunca,sombra que sempre me asombras.

You are in all, and you are all,for me, and in my very self you dwell,nor will you ever leave me,shadow that always haunts me.

Artifact70.3 cm high

Bell (Lai zhong)

China, Shaanxi province, Meixian · Western Zhou dynasty · c. 800–700 BCE · bronze · 70.3 × 37 × 26.6 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art 1989.3 · CC0

Begu’s bell rang for a death; this one was cast for one. It’s the second largest of a set of eight, and its 118-character inscription says why. A man named Lai, granted a hereditary position, had the bells made for his late father, Gongshu. The king’s command to him: “Manage the fisheries and forests of the realm.” Then, in Cleveland’s translation, the bells name their own sound:

a set of harmonized bells. Ding, dong, bing, bong, ding, dong, bing, bong. May they be used in acts of piety, reverently gladdening past luminaries.

Cleveland notes that the bell’s unusual lens-shaped cavity let a single bell sound two different tones.

PaintingValencia, c. 1400

The Coronation of the Virgin with the Trinity

Master of Rubielos de Mora (Spanish) · c. 1400 · oil on panel · 144.6 × 91.4 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art 1947.208 · CC0

Begu saw a soul carried up by angels; here, about 1400 in Valencia, is the arrival. Cleveland’s description: “Surrounded by a choir of music-making angels, the Virgin kneels to accept her crown from God and Christ.” The dove of the Holy Ghost touches its wings to their mouths. The painter, named for an altarpiece at Rubielos de Mora in Teruel, has the local style: “piercing eyes and protruding mouths with long slender fingers.”

Words by Rosalía de Castro; music by Xoán Montes · on Núñez’s Todos os mundos (2000) · also on the soundtrack of Mar adentro (2004)

Delaware: a duty that can’t be handed off

In Leiske v. Kidd, Vice Chancellor Lori Will sanctioned a partner $2,500, payable personally, for a brief with AI-fabricated quotations. She spared his firm for its AI policy and training. She also wrote that “[a] fake quotation or a fabricated legal synthesis generated by an algorithm is not ‘existing law,’” and that a lawyer’s duty of candor and duty of reasonable inquiry “are personal and nondelegable.” Not to a paralegal, then, and not to a machine.
