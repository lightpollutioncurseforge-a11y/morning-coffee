AnecdotePacific, 20 November 1820

“We have been stove by a whale”

Two hundred and six years ago today, at 0° 40′ S, 119° W, the Nantucket whaleship Essex lowered boats for a pod of sperm whales. The first mate, Owen Chase, was back aboard mending a damaged boat when a very large bull rammed the ship, passed under it, and came again. Chase’s account, published the next year:

I turned around, and saw him about one hundred rods directly ahead of us, coming down apparently with twice his ordinary speed, and to me at that moment, it appeared with tenfold fury and vengeance in his aspect.Owen Chase, Narrative (1821)

When Captain Pollard’s boat came back, he sat “pale and speechless,” then asked, “My God, Mr. Chase, what is the matter?” Chase: “We have been stove by a whale.”

Chase concluded it was “anything but chance”: two attacks, both head-on, from “an animal, too, never before suspected of premeditated violence.” Nathaniel Philbrick later guessed the hammering on the boat had drawn it. Nobody knows.

Then came the choice of course. The Society Islands, Chase wrote, “if inhabited, we presumed they were by savages, from whom we had as much to fear as from the elements.” They steered for South America instead, about 2,000 nautical miles. Melville later wrote: “But they dreaded cannibals.” In February, in the captain’s boat, they drew lots, and Owen Coffin was shot and eaten. Five of the seventeen who left the island they landed on lived to be picked up. Pollard, Wikipedia says, spent every 20 November afterwards fasting, locked in his room.

One wrinkle: Wikipedia has Pollard favouring the Society Islands and Chase and Joy talking him out of it. Chase’s own narrative presents it as a joint decision.

On Leviathan!, Topic, 1967 · with Alf Edwards (English concertina), Dave Swarbrick (fiddle) and Martin Carthy (mandolin)

The Pacific whale fishery’s own song, from the grounds the Essex was working. Lloyd recorded it three times; this is the last. His note calls it “the most important ballad of the South-Seamen,” and thinks it may be a right whale, not a sperm. The Ian Campbell Folk Group were blunter: “A stark song about an everyday incident in a bloody and wasteful trade.” For your liking for old narrative song.

NetChoice, L.L.C. v. Fitch

CourtFifth Circuit, No. 25-60348 · Nov. 19, 2026
HoldingVacated and remanded. NetChoice hadn’t shown that Mississippi’s Walker Montgomery Protecting Children Online Act is unconstitutional as applied to each member’s site, and the injunction was not tailored to match.
Vote3–0. Wilson for Richman, Engelhardt and himself. No separate opinions.

The 2024 law requires age verification, parental consent for minors’ accounts, and steps to reduce harm to minors. After Moody v. NetChoice made facial challenges hard, NetChoice won a second injunction covering nine named platforms. The panel calls it facial relief by another name:

In short, an as-applied injunction is necessarily a limited one, constrained by the specifics of the factual record.
Rather, it is strikingly facial.

NetChoice must now prove its case “member-by-member, function-by-function, and statutory section-by-section.” Using X as its example, the court lists the feeds, messaging, ads, shopping and “Grok, X’s standalone AI feature,” and says the record doesn’t show which of them the Act reaches. A footnote recalls that the Supreme Court let the stay stand; Justice Kavanaugh thought NetChoice likely to win on the merits but not on the balance of harms. The law can be enforced meanwhile.

Texas Chemistry Council v. EPA

CourtFifth Circuit, Nos. 24-60193 & 24-60281 · Nov. 19, 2026
HoldingPetitions dismissed. No one challenging EPA’s 2024 ban on chrysotile asbestos uses had standing, so the rule stands, unreviewed.
Vote3–0. Engelhardt for Clement, Southwick and himself.

Industry groups said the rule went too far; public-health groups and firefighters said it didn’t go far enough. Both sides lost on the same rule. The industry side and the steelworkers didn’t put proof of standing in their opening briefs, and the firefighters showed only an “increased risk.” Engelhardt:

But on direct review of agency actions, we don’t fill in the blanks for petitioners or give them the benefit of the doubt. We require evidence.

Petitioners may add evidence to the record, “but it must be done at the time of their opening brief,” not after the court starts asking.

Poem · FrenchLes Fleurs du mal, 1861

Charles Baudelaire, L’Homme et la Mer

Complete, 16 lines · text of the 1861 edition (Poulet-Malassis et de Broise, pp. 36–37), from Wikisource · translation by Claude, unrhymed

Chase gave the whale intent. Baudelaire gives the sea and the man the same depths and the same appetite, and calls them brothers at war.

Homme libre, toujours tu chériras la mer !La mer est ton miroir ; tu contemples ton âmeDans le déroulement infini de sa lame,Et ton esprit n’est pas un gouffre moins amer.

Free man, you will always cherish the sea!The sea is your mirror; you contemplate your soulin the endless unrolling of its wave,and your mind is a gulf no less bitter.

Tu te plais à plonger au sein de ton image ;Tu l’embrasses des yeux et des bras, et ton cœurSe distrait quelquefois de sa propre rumeurAu bruit de cette plainte indomptable et sauvage.

You love to plunge into the heart of your image;you embrace it with eyes and arms, and your heartis sometimes distracted from its own murmurby the sound of that untamable, savage lament.

Vous êtes tous les deux ténébreux et discrets :Homme, nul n’a sondé le fond de tes abîmes ;Ô mer, nul ne connaît tes richesses intimes,Tant vous êtes jaloux de garder vos secrets !

You are both of you dark and close-mouthed:man, no one has sounded the floor of your depths;O sea, no one knows your hidden riches,so jealous are you both of keeping your secrets!

Et cependant voilà des siècles innombrablesQue vous vous combattez sans pitié ni remord,Tellement vous aimez le carnage et la mort,Ô lutteurs éternels, ô frères implacables !

And yet for countless centuries nowyou have fought each other without pity or remorse,so much do you love carnage and death,O eternal wrestlers, O implacable brothers!

SongParis, 27 March 1957

Léo Ferré, “La Vie antérieure”

Baudelaire’s words, Ferré’s music · on Les Fleurs du mal, Odeon, 1957 · 3:21

More Baudelaire, sung. French Wikipedia calls Ferré’s 1957 album probably the first in French chanson given wholly to one poet. Odeon doubted it would sell, so Ferré gave up his usual advances to get it made. The budget ran to two sessions with a band. On the third day he recorded this and three others with no one but himself and Janine de Waleyne on the ondes Martenot. A deep cut to set beside the poem above, not a sure thing.

ArtifactCoast of Peru, 200–850 CE

Sea lion pup vessel

Moche, North Coast of Peru · 200–850 CE · ceramic and slip · 19.7 × 15.5 × 16 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art 2014.375 · CC0

Another hunted sea creature, from the coast the ballad names. Moche art shows sea lions as effigies like this one, or as “targets of human hunters.” They may have been prized for the beach pebbles in their stomachs, which modern Peruvian folk healers treat as powerful medicine. And colonial-period natives believed sea lions “carried the dead to off-shore islands,” an idea Cleveland thinks may go back to the Moche. You saved a Moche stirrup-spout bottle; this is the gentler side.

AIAuthority and law

Strasbourg bars a lawyer over AI-invented case law

On 13 November, according to CEA Digital Law, the President of the European Court of Human Rights barred a Romanian lawyer for two years from representing or assisting applicants there. Her AI-drafted submissions cited non-existent judgments, invented paragraphs of real ones, and real cases that were irrelevant or cut against her. She admitted using AI and called the problems “inaccuracies” and “citation errors.” The Court treated them as repeated failings, not slips. I couldn’t find the Court’s own decision or a case number, so this rests on that one report.

Arizona opens a criminal investigation into AI firms

Arizona Attorney General Kris Mayes said on Wednesday that the state has opened a criminal investigation into OpenAI and other AI companies over the “apparent infiltration of banks by rogue AI agents,” and whether state law is being broken on an ongoing basis. She named neither the other companies nor the banks. “The state of Arizona is very worried about this, and we are looking at it from multiple angles,” she said. It came two days after California sued OpenAI. An investigation doesn’t mean charges.

The bishops vote on AI investments (follow-up)

Yesterday I couldn’t confirm that the U.S. bishops’ new guidelines exclude autonomous weapons. Religion News Service now reports the vote, on 18 November, was overwhelming. The conference may not invest in firms making autonomous weapons or automated targeting “for the military, security or immigration enforcement” without meaningful human oversight, or AI designed to be addictive or manipulative. Only the conference itself is bound by them, though many dioceses follow them.
