Approaching Thunder Storm

Martin Johnson Heade (American, 1819–1904) · 1859 · oil on canvas · 71.1 × 111.8 cm · Metropolitan Museum 1975.160

This is Narragansett Bay, Rhode Island, from a sketch Heade made of a real storm. The fisherman has a dog, an iron kettle and a sail spread out on the shore. A second man rows in, having left his boat out on the bay, and there’s a faint red bolt of lightning at the left. It was Heade’s largest painting to date, and historians have connected it to the tensions before the Civil War.

He shared a studio with Frederic Church, and the long horizon is Church’s. Your saved images favour orange against blue at golden hour; this is the opposite weather, almost all slate.

AnecdoteNight of 26–27 November 1703, Old Style

A Man of so much Charity in a Town of so little

On 26 November 1703, by the Julian calendar England still used, a storm crossed south Wales and southern England. Wikipedia counts about 1,500 seamen drowned, many on the Goodwin Sands. Daniel Defoe gathered letters from witnesses and published The Storm the next July. He knew the limits of his own book:

No Pen can describe it, no Tongue can express it, no Thought conceive it, unless some of those who were in the Extremity of itDefoe, The Storm (1704)

Off Plymouth stood Henry Winstanley’s lighthouse on the Eddystone reef. Winstanley, an engraver with a taste for mechanical gadgets, was out on the rock that night making repairs. Defoe learned only “that at Night it was standing, and in the Morning all the upper part from the Gallery was blown down, and all the People in it perished.” He adds a story he heard: at the same moment the model of the lighthouse in Winstanley’s house at Littlebury, “above 200 Miles from the Light-House, fell down, and was broken to pieces.” He gives it as hearsay, and so do I.

The better story is at Deal. Men from the wrecked warships were stranded on the Goodwin Sands at low water, signalling to the shore and waiting for the tide to wash them “into another World.” Boats went out, Defoe says, “in quest of Booty.” The mayor, Thomas Powell, a slop-seller, asked the Custom House officers for their boats and was “rudely refus’d.” So he offered five shillings a head, out of his own pocket, for every man saved, and took the Custom House boats by force, though “he knew he could not justify it.” More than 200 men came off the sands.

The Queen’s agent for sick and wounded seamen then refused to “Disburse one Penny,” so Powell fed and lodged the survivors and buried those who died, at his own charge. He was repaid eventually, after “great Obstructions and Delays.” Defoe calls him “a Man of so much Charity in a Town of so little.”

Artifact29 cm, a river stone

Tlaloc

Aztec · Central Mexico · c. 1200–1519 · greenstone · 29 × 19.5 × 13.5 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art 1966.361

The carver kept the shape of a natural cobble, smoothed it, and cut the rain god into it in low relief. You can tell it’s Tlaloc by the ringed eyes, the twisted nose element and the fangs, and he holds a stalk of maize. Cleveland notes that to the Aztecs he was already an ancient god, whose worship went back to the ruins of Teotihuacan. You’ve saved bronze faces that stare back. This one does it in green stone.

Two courts on what the common law still decides

Laskar v. Hurd

CourtEleventh Circuit, No. 25-11296 · Nov. 23, 2026
HoldingAffirmed. Georgia Tech audit officials who reported a professor’s suspected misconduct didn’t violate the Fourth Amendment, because the warrant affidavit “depended solely on an independent investigation and not the university officials’ report.”
Vote3–0. Chief Judge William Pryor wrote for Lagoa and Kidd, and also concurred alone.

Joy Laskar ran a microchip company on the side, and an audit found it had used university resources. The charges were later dismissed on limitations grounds, and he sued. The concurrence is the part to read. Justices Thomas, Gorsuch and Alito have questioned treating malicious prosecution as the common-law model for these claims. Pryor says the majority in Thompson v. Clark “never provided a fulsome explanation” for choosing it, and draws on Blackstone, Cooley and Johnstone v. Sutton (1786) to supply one:

That explanation has not satisfied critics. … And their criticisms merit a response.
To the extent that the two claims are not perfectly coterminous, any mismatch is only the inevitable byproduct of comparing two similar but not identical concepts.

King v. Villegas

CourtNinth Circuit en banc, No. 23-1713 · Nov. 24, 2026
HoldingA prisoner who pleaded nolo contendere to resisting an officer can still sue the officers for excessive force. Heck v. Humphrey doesn’t bar the claim, and Sanders v. City of Pittsburg (2021) is overruled insofar as it held otherwise.
Vote9–2 to reverse. W. Fletcher wrote the opinion. Owens, with Miller and Forrest, concurred only in the judgment. Collins dissented, joined by VanDyke, who also dissented alone.

Jerry Lee King fought with two guards at Kern Valley State Prison in 2016 and sued. More than a year after the fight, the state charged him, and he pleaded no contest. Fletcher’s point is what a nolo plea is, quoting North Carolina v. Alford: “a consent by the defendant that he may be punished as if he were guilty and a prayer for leniency.” Even the concurrence is alarmed. Owens calls the holding “a dramatic change in how judges have applied this doctrine for decades.” VanDyke is blunter:

Unfortunately, this court doesn’t like Heck and often works hard to avoid it.

Poem · Russian1832

Парус (The Sail)

Mikhail Lermontov (1814–1841) · complete, 12 lines · text from the autograph, as printed on Russian Wikisource · translation by Claude, unrhymed

Heade’s lone white sail is the picture for it. Wikisource’s note dates the poem between the first half of August and 2 September 1832, when Lermontov copied it into a letter to Maria Lopukhina. Its first line matches a line in Bestuzhev-Marlinsky’s Andrei, Prince of Pereyaslavl, published anonymously in 1828. The last two lines are the ones to keep.

Белеет парус одинокойВ тумане моря голубом!..Что ищет он в стране далекой?Что кинул он в краю родном?..

A lone sail shows whitein the blue mist of the sea!..What is it seeking in a far country?What did it cast off in its own?..

Играют волны — ветер свищет,И мачта гнется и скрыпит...Увы! он счастия не ищет,И не от счастия бежит!

The waves play, the wind whistles,and the mast bends and creaks…Alas! it is not looking for happiness,and not running from happiness!

Под ним струя светлей лазури,Над ним луч солнца золотой...А он, мятежный, просит бури,Как будто в бурях есть покой!

Beneath it, a current brighter than azure;above it, a golden ray of sun…But it, rebellious, begs for storms,as if in storms there were peace!

Painting1.7 metres of silk

River Village in a Rainstorm

Lü Wenying (Chinese, court painter in the Hongzhi era) · c. 1480–1507 · hanging scroll; ink and slight colour on silk · 170.5 × 103.4 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art 1970.76

Heade watches the storm come in; Lü puts you inside it, with rain driven in diagonal sheets across the whole scroll. He served at court in the Hongzhi era (1488–1505) and is, in Cleveland’s words, “hardly known, with few remaining works.” He belongs to the Zhe school, which turned Southern Song poetic scenery into large, dramatic views. Look low in the village: a boy peeks out of a window, perhaps at two people struggling through the rain.

Artifact37 cm of bronze

The Wrath of Neptune

Cast after a model by Tiziano Minio (Italian, 1511/12–1552) · late 1500s–early 1600s · bronze · 36.7 × 17.2 × 19.4 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art 1974.273

This is the scene from Aeneid I. Juno has set the winds on the Trojan fleet, and Neptune, furious that anyone stirred his sea without him, rides out. The empty right hand once held a trident and is stretched out to still the waves. It was once given to Sansovino; Minio, his pupil, used the same grooved waves on a relief for the Loggetta in Venice. Virgil has Neptune scold the winds for daring this meo sine numine, without my say, and then break off his own threat:

Quos ego—sed motos praestat componere fluctus.Aeneid I.135 · “You whom I— but better first to calm the waves you’ve stirred.” Translation by Claude

PhotographPlymouth, 1890s

Eddystone, two towers

Detroit Publishing Co. photochrom, print no. 10275 · between ca. 1890 and ca. 1900 · Library of Congress LC-DIG-ppmsc-08791 · no known restrictions

This is the reef where Winstanley’s tower stood. The tall one is the fourth light, James Douglass’s granite tower, first lit in 1882. The stump at left is the base of John Smeaton’s third tower, whose upper rooms were taken down and rebuilt on Plymouth Hoe. Douglass hoped “the rock below will for ages endure to support this portion of Smeaton’s lighthouse.” The two bells on the gallery were fog signals, struck by the clockwork that turned the lens. As for the night of the storm, Defoe says that soon after the first tower fell, the Winchelsea, homeward bound from Virginia, “was split upon the Rock, where that Building stood.”

SongLebanon, after September 1972

Fairuz, “Saalouni n’Nass”

“The People Asked Me” · from the Rahbani musical Al Mahatta · music by Ziad Rahbani, words by Mansour Rahbani · I couldn’t confirm the recording’s release year

On 22 September 1972 Fairuz’s husband, Assi Rahbani, had a brain haemorrhage. Their son Ziad, 17, gave her a tune he had written for another singer in his first play. His uncle Mansour wrote new words for it, about Fairuz on stage for the first time without Assi. Three months after the haemorrhage, Assi was in the audience at the premiere of Al Mahatta at the Piccadilly Theatre on Hamra Street. Assi’s younger brother Elias, whom you’ve liked on his own, arranged the music for that performance.

AIMachines and authority

Leo XIV: life and death “entrusted to machines”

Addressing the Dicastery for Interreligious Dialogue on Thursday, the pope listed AI among the new threats to peace:

Peace is also at risk due to further technological advances that promote the militarization of artificial intelligence, with decisions concerning life and death being increasingly entrusted to machines

His answer is “integral disarmament,” which he says “cannot be achieved only through political agreements or the reduction of weapons alone.”

An Australian judge bans AI from one case’s filings

In Kavanagh v Atlassian [2026] FCA 1774, decided 20 November, Justice Perry found that some citations by a self-represented software engineer suing his employer “did not exist, or contained other errors.” He appeared to accept that he had used generative AI, at least in part. She ordered: “Save with leave of the Court, no party is to use generative artificial intelligence to produce text in any document filed with the Court, including submissions and affidavits.” The order applies to the parties in this case only. I read the judgment from Damien Charlotin’s database, not the court’s own site.

The FCA: the agent’s acts are the firm’s

Britain’s Financial Conduct Authority said on 24 November that a firm using AI agents, its own or a third party’s, keeps responsibility, and so do its senior managers. It warns the platforms too: “Just because a business describes itself as a technology provider does not mean that they do not need to be authorised by us to provide financial services in the UK.”
