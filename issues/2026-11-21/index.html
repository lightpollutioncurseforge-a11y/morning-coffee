Scholar Reclining and Watching Rising Clouds Poem by Wang Wei

Ma Lin (Chinese, c. 1185–after 1260) · Southern Song, c. 1256 · album leaf, ink on silk · image 25.1 × 25.3 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art 1961.421 · CC0

The clouds. The idea came from the emperor. Painting and poem were once mounted together as one fan. Emperor Lizong wrote out a couplet by the Tang poet Wang Wei, and Ma Lin answered it in paint. Cleveland translates the couplet: “I walk unto where waters end / And sit down to watch when clouds arise.” A gourd-shaped seal gives the cyclical date bingchen, 1256, and the signature reads “Your servant, Ma Lin.”

AnecdoteParis, 21 November 1783

“Est-ce que vous dansez ?”

Two hundred and forty-three years ago today, Pilâtre de Rozier and the marquis d’Arlandes left the garden of the Château de la Muette in a Montgolfier hot-air balloon. No person had flown free before. On the first try the wind caught it while still tethered and tore it. Ladies in the crowd offered to help sew it up, and an hour and a half later they tried again.

D’Arlandes’ own account ran in the Journal de Paris. The two men stood in a gallery on opposite sides of the fire, feeding it straw. The crowd went silent, so he waved his handkerchief. Then his partner:

Vous ne faites rien et nous ne montons guère.“You’re doing nothing, and we’re hardly going up.” Pilâtre, per d’Arlandes

Later, a jolt from above. D’Arlandes: “Que faites-vous ? Est-ce que vous dansez ?” (“What are you doing? Are you dancing?”) Pilâtre: “Je ne bouge pas” (“I’m not moving”). The fire was burning round holes in the cloth. D’Arlandes put them out with a wet sponge, struck the main ropes with it to test them, and decided they could cross Paris after all. They came down at the Butte aux Cailles, between two windmills. The balloon collapsed over Pilâtre, who crawled out in his shirtsleeves. He had stowed his coat in his basket, and the crowd seized the coat and shared it out.

Benjamin Franklin watched from his terrace. D’Arlandes gives the start as “une heure cinquante-quatre minutes”; the Linda Hall Library has 12:54. The Met holds Clodion’s terracotta model for a monument to the invention of the balloon, about 1784, but its image server refused me.

Association, and a single passenger

Shilling v. County of San Diego

CourtNinth Circuit, No. 25-291 · Nov. 20, 2026
HoldingReversed and remanded. The county sheriff is not an “arm of the state” and can’t claim sovereign immunity. Revoking the licence was county policy. The Second Amendment claim is not moot. And the First Amendment association claim was wrongly dismissed.
Vote3–0. Bumatay for Lee, Sung and himself. No separate opinions.

In December 2023 the sheriff revoked Kenneth Shilling’s concealed-carry licence under California’s old “good moral character” test because he belongs to the Boozefighters Motorcycle Club. Her letter called the club a “criminal street gang.” Shilling has no criminal record and says the club, founded in 1946 by World War II veterans, does charity work. On association, the panel quotes the Supreme Court’s First Choice decision this year: without the right, government could “reduce any assembly to a party of one.” It adds, quoting Healy v. James:

This kind of “guilt by association alone, without establishing that an individual’s association poses the threat feared by the Government, is an impermissible basis upon which to deny First Amendment rights.”

Unless the sheriff can show the club “possessed unlawful goals” and that Shilling meant to further them, she cannot punish him for his affiliation.

A.M.A.S. v. Mullin

CourtNinth Circuit, No. 26-6950 · Nov. 20, 2026
HoldingStay pending appeal granted. The government need not, for now, facilitate the return of an Egyptian man it deported to Equatorial Guinea.
Vote2–1. Per curiam (Bennett and H.A. Thomas). Graber dissents in large part.

The order is one paragraph: the four Nken factors “support the issuance of a stay.” Graber’s dissent supplies the facts, from the district court’s findings. The man was a permanent resident, and a judge had deferred his removal to Egypt because he would likely be tortured there. In April he was sent instead to Equatorial Guinea, without a chance to object. There he was held at a decommissioned hotel and beaten, and filmed with guns aimed at him. In September he was arrested and held incommunicado. The district court ordered the government to “facilitate” his return, the remedy the Supreme Court approved in Abrego Garcia.

Graber would stay only the later orders that “micromanag[e]” the government’s efforts. The government relied on the Supreme Court’s unexplained stay in D.V.D., a class case over thousands of removals. She calls that comparison something that “strains credulity”:

Whatever harm could result from arranging a flight for one passenger, none of the harms described in D.V.D. is present.
There is no possible irreparable harm here. In my view, it is not even a close call.

Artifact66.7 cm tall

Thunder God (Raijin)

Japan, Kamakura period · 1300s · wood with traces of gesso and red and black pigment · 66.7 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art 1972.64 · CC0

The scholar’s opposite. Cleveland says the thunder and wind gods first appear in Buddhist art among demon-monsters flying through the sky above the meditating Buddha, “in an attempt to frighten away his concentration.” Raijin was once ringed with small drums, which he struck with a stick; a metal clasp on this one’s back held the ring. His partner, the wind god Fujin, is in the Fukuoka Art Museum. Your artifacts folder keeps faces that stare back. This one is mid-stride, shouting.

Poem · ChineseTang, 8th c.

Wang Wei, 終南別業 · My Retreat Below Zhongnan

Complete, 8 lines · text from Wikisource, citing the Quan Tangshi, juan 126 · translation by Claude, unrhymed

The source of the emperor’s couplet in the lead painting. Wikisource records two other titles for the poem and variant characters in lines 4, 7 and 8; I print its main reading.

中歲頗好道，晚家南山陲。興來每獨往，勝事空自知。

In middle age I grew fond of the Way;late, I made my home at the foot of the Southern Mountains.When the mood comes I go off alone,and the fine things only I know.

行到水窮處，坐看雲起時。偶然值林叟，談笑無還期。

I walk to where the water ends,and sit and watch the time the clouds rise.By chance I meet an old man of the woods;we talk and laugh and set no hour for going home.

SongMandarin, 1983

Teresa Teng, “但願人長久” (Wishing We Last Forever)

Music by Liang Hongzhi, arranged by Xiao Weichen · on 淡淡幽情 (Dandan youqing), Polydor, released 2 February 1983 · 4:06

Teng’s album set twelve classical poems of the Tang and Song to new music; this was the first single. The words are Su Shi’s “Water Song,” written at the Mid-Autumn festival of 1076; its preface says he drank till dawn, got very drunk, and was thinking of Ziyou. Partway through, he asks the sky about the moon and wants to go up:

我欲乘風歸去，惟恐瓊樓玉宇，高處不勝寒。“I would ride the wind home, but fear the jade towers and marble halls: so high up, the cold is more than I could bear.” Su Shi, as printed on Wikisource, which notes a variant 又 for 惟. Translation mine.

Chinese-language vintage pop is in your likes. Wikipedia reports over five million copies sold in Asia, so this is the known name; the reason is the line above.

PaintingSaint-Clair, c. 1896

The Pink Cloud

Henri-Edmond Cross (French, 1856–1910) · c. 1896 · oil on canvas · 54.6 × 61 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art 2020.106 · CC0

The loud cloud, after the quiet one. Rheumatism drove Cross from Paris in 1891 to Saint-Clair on the Mediterranean. He wrote to his mother: “I have enough here to keep me busy all of life—I have just discovered happiness.” There he took up pointillism, dots of complementary colour mixing in the eye. Cleveland points to the pair of cypresses that tie the garden to the sky. You saved a pointillist suburb among cypresses; this is a sky full of the same weather.

Mustafa Özkent, “Burçak Tarlaları”

On Gençlik İle Elele, Evren, 1973 · Grafson Studios, Istanbul · 2:56 · reissued by Finders Keepers, 2006

The October list of deeper Turkish cuts had this name on it. Özkent was a session guitarist and arranger; Andy Votel called him the “Dr. Frankenstein of Anatolian pop.” The album went largely unnoticed until the 2006 reissue. It is all instrumentals, with Özkent’s arrangements of songs like this one. PopMatters heard “half an hour of wordless Turkish psychedelica chilled to a nice consistency.” I haven’t heard it and can’t tell you which track is best; treat this one as a door into the album.

Cloud-Collar Pillow with Waves

China, Jiangxi Province · Northern Song, 1000s–1100s · glazed porcelain with incised and carved decoration · 10 × 17.5 × 13.4 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art 2003.88 · CC0

Somewhere to lay your head after watching clouds: a pillow in the shape of one, a ruyi head. Cleveland says the hard porcelain was the point: it “kept the user cool as they slept.”

AIAuthority and law

An expert’s prompts are part of “the basis” of her opinion

On Friday Judge Vernon Oliver in Connecticut upheld an order that Naomi Oreskes, the Harvard historian of science and an expert for the Conservation Law Foundation in its suit over a Shell-linked fuel terminal in New Haven, must disclose the AI prompts her team used. Her assistant ran the defendants’ documents through ChatGPT on a dedicated server and didn’t keep complete logs. At deposition she said she used “AI as a sieve to identify all the documents that could be relevant to a particular question.” Oliver held that the prompts “comprise ‘the basis’” for her opinions under Rule 26, and are not protected like draft notes. To show what a prompt is, the court quotes ChatGPT’s own help page. Compliance is due by 18 December. The tool here is OpenAI’s.

“As opposed to gods, animals, or machines”

Cambridge Dictionary has made “human” its word of the year. Its definition: “having the qualities, faults, and feelings that people have, as opposed to gods, animals, or machines.” The lexicographer Colin McIntosh said the word “has traditionally distinguished us from gods, animals, and, latterly, from machines,” and pointed to new compounds such as “human-made.”
