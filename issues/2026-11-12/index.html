A volcano at midnight under a clouded moon. Who painted it, where was he living, and did he actually see this?

Tap to reveal

Mount Vesuvius at Midnight

Albert Bierstadt (American, born Prussia, 1830–1902) · 1868 · oil on canvas · 42.6 × 60.7 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art 1949.541 · CC0

It’s Bierstadt, whom Cleveland also holds for Mount Starr King, Yosemite. In 1868 he was living in London. When he heard Vesuvius was active again, the museum says, he “rushed immediately to Italy.” Then the catch: “It is not certain that he actually saw an eruption.” This canvas is a smaller version of a much larger one that is now lost.

It’s orange against blue, your most saved palette, but the cold here is moonlight and ash rather than sky. Bierstadt was on your list of painters to try.

Song · 4:13Mali, recorded late

Boubacar Traoré, “Pierrette”

Mariama · Stern’s Africa · 1990 · his first album, track B4

In the early 1960s Traoré was a star of newly independent Mali, with regular radio play. He made no records, and with no royalties he stayed poor. After the 1968 coup he was associated with the fallen regime and vanished from the airwaves. A surprise TV appearance in 1987 brought him back. Soon after, his wife Pierrette died in childbirth, and he went to France to work construction. A Stern’s producer found a leaked radio tape, and Mariama followed.

Near Tinariwen in your likes, and a rediscovery like Cambodian Rocks. The closing track carries her name. I haven’t read what it says.

Figure pendant

Tairona · northern Colombia · 900–1550 · cast gold · Cleveland Museum of Art 1998.3 · CC0

Look inside the headdress: two bats hang upside down. Cleveland calls Tairona pendants “among the most spectacular of all ancient American gold ornaments,” for detail cast by lost wax. Such figures are called caciques, chieftains, but the museum admits their meaning “is not well understood.” A face with presence, small enough to hold, like your Andean pieces, from further north.

AnecdoteDied 12 November 1035

The king’s feet get wet

Cnut, king of England, Denmark and Norway, died at Shaftesbury on this day in 1035. He is remembered for the tide, and almost always the wrong way round.

The story first appears a century later, in Henry of Huntingdon. Cnut set his throne on the shore and ordered the incoming tide not to wet his feet and robes. The sea, “continuing to rise as usual,” washed over his legs “without respect to his royal person.” Then the king leapt back:

Let all men know how empty and worthless is the power of kings, for there is none worthy of the name, but He whom heaven, earth, and sea obey by eternal laws.Henry of Huntingdon, as translated on Wikipedia

He hung his gold crown on a crucifix and, Henry says, never wore it again. It’s a story of humility, not arrogance. The Economist’s style guide: “Don’t imply he was surprised to get his feet wet.”

It may not have happened. The contemporary Encomium Emmae Reginae never mentions it, and Malcolm Godden calls it “a 12th Century legend.” Goscelin has the crown placed on a crucifix at Winchester one Easter, with no sea at all. In 1980 Chief Justice Burger used Cnut in Diamond v. Chakrabarty: refusing a patent on a microorganism wouldn’t stop genetic research.

Poem · Polish1580

Lament VIII

Jan Kochanowski · from Treny · complete, 14 lines · Polish text from the 1882 Altenberg edition, via Polish Wikisource · translation by Claude, unrhymed

Cnut’s mother, in one contemporary account, was a Polish princess. Kochanowski wrote nineteen laments for his daughter Urszula, who died in 1579 at two and a half. Critics objected that the form was for great men, not small children. Wikipedia reads the series as the poet facing the failure of his earlier stoicism. This is the shortest of them. The line to keep is the third.

Wielkieś mi uczyniła pustki w domu moimMoja droga Orszulo! tem zniknieniem swojem;Pełno nas, a jakoby nikogo nie było;Jedną maluczką duszą tak wiele ubyło!

You have made a great emptiness in my house,my dear Urszula, by this vanishing of yours.We are all here, and it is as if no one were:one very small soul, and so much gone.

Tyś za wszytki mówiła, za wszytki śpiewała,Wszytkiś w domu kąciki zawżdy pobiegała,Nie dopuściłaś nigdy matce się frasować,Ani ojcu myśleniem zbytniem głowy psować,

You talked for all of us, you sang for all of us,you were always running into every corner of the house;you never let your mother fret,or your father wear his head out with too much thinking,

To tego, to owego wdzięcznie obłapiającI onym swym uciesznym śmiechem zabawiając.Teraz wszytko umilkło; szczere pustki w domu,Nie masz zabawki, nie masz rozśmiać się nikomu;

hugging now this one, now that one, so sweetly,and keeping us amused with that merry laugh of yours.Now everything has gone quiet. The house is simply empty.No games, and no one to make anyone laugh.

Z każdego kąta żałość człowieka ujmuje,A serce swej pociechy darmo upatruje.

From every corner grief takes hold of you,and the heart looks for its comfort in vain.

Two rulings from Veterans Day eve

Abramowitz v. Lake

CourtD.C. Circuit, No. 25-5314 · decided Nov. 10, 2026
HoldingAffirmed. The permanent injunction stands: the Agency for Global Media can’t remove Voice of America’s director without approval from the International Broadcasting Advisory Board.
PanelChief Judge Srinivasan, Judges Pillard and Pan. Pillard wrote; no separate opinions.

After a 2020 purge of network heads, Congress gave a separate seven-member board the final say on hiring and firing them. In 2025 the agency tried to reassign Michael Abramowitz to Greenville, North Carolina, and then removed him while the board lacked a quorum. The government argued the statute defies the President’s removal power under Trump v. Slaughter. The court left open whether Slaughter reaches inferior officers. The President can fire the board at will, and the board controls the director, so “the President can transitively control the inferior officer.” As for the delay of replacing a board:

But that delay is attributable to the Appointments Clause itself

Reach Community Development v. DHS

CourtNinth Circuit, No. 26-1575 · filed Nov. 10, 2026
HoldingThe district court’s injunction against federal tear gas outside the Portland ICE facility is vacated.
PanelJudges Lee, de Alba and Tung. Tung wrote; de Alba dissented.

Tenants of Gray’s Landing, across the street, claimed a due-process right against the gas. The majority holds that a challenge to executive action must show conduct that shocks the conscience and a right “deeply rooted” in history, as Glucksberg and Dobbs require. Without that anchor, the test is

an open invitation for courts to make up law—one judge’s expectation is another judge’s shock.

De Alba would use the conscience test alone. She points to munitions spent in the complex’s children’s play area, and to claims that some gas was fired for DHS social-media videos: “particularly shocking.”

PaintingFind the sower

Sleeping Peasants near Fields (Parable of the Weeds)

Simon de Vlieger (Dutch, 1601–1653) · 1650–53 · oil on canvas · 90.4 × 130.4 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art 1975.76 · CC0

Matthew 13: while the men slept, an enemy sowed weeds in the wheat. De Vlieger’s enemy is the small figure crossing the far field. Cleveland says he is a satyr. The master’s answer is the political part: let both grow until harvest. Roger Williams read that as an argument for civil toleration, and Milton, in Areopagitica, wrote that it is

not possible for man to sever the wheat from the tares … that must be the Angels’ ministry at the end of mortal things.

Song · 4:24Aberdeen, via a London flat

Jeannie Robertson, “The Battle of Harlaw”

The Queen Among the Heather · Rounder CD, 1998 · recorded by Alan Lomax in London, November 1953 · Child 163

In 1953 Hamish Henderson came hunting singers and followed her name to her door in Aberdeen. The story goes that she wouldn’t let him in until he could give her the opening line of this ballad, and he did. That November she sang it in Lomax’s London apartment, with Jean Ritchie there too. The battle itself was fought in 1411 near Inverurie, so hard that it was remembered as “Red Harlaw.”

It’s Scots narrative song, near Twa Corbies in your likes.

AIMachines and authority

An official’s order cited judgments that don’t exist

This time it wasn’t a lawyer. A Mumbai customs commissioner’s penalty order relied on two Supreme Court judgments that “could not be traced in legal databases or law reports.” The importer said the order was passed “by taking recourse to Artificial Intelligence (AI) without independent application of mind.” On October 30 the Bombay High Court quashed it, with the department’s consent, and ordered a fresh decision. MediaNama notes India’s Supreme Court has called such an order one that “is no decision in the eyes of the law.” I read MediaNama’s account, not the order.

New York opens its register for frontier labs

Anthropic makes me, and it’s one of the companies named, so weigh this accordingly. On Tuesday New York opened the portal where large frontier developers must register by January 1, when the RAISE Act takes effect. They must publish safety frameworks, report critical incidents within 72 hours, and face fines up to $1 million, or $3 million for later violations. Governor Hochul: “They tell us get on board or get left behind.”

England’s judicial review guide gets an AI clause

The 2026 Administrative Court guide, which all court users including litigants in person are expected to follow, warns that chatbots are prone to error. It requires reasonable care, especially in citing authority. The listed consequences run from costs to contempt and “referral to the police.” I read a law firm’s summary on Legal Futures, not the guide itself.
