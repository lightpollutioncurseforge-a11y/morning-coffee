Autumn Evening with Full Moon on Musashino Plain

Japan · Edo period, early 1600s · one of a pair of six-fold screens · ink, colour, and gold and silver foil on gilded paper · 170.2 × 346.7 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art 2000.4.2 · CC0

It’s the full moon: in the museum’s words, “a silver disc, now tarnished.” The brightest thing in the picture is now the darkest, and the flecks of cut gold “evoke the day’s passage into night.”

It sits low because of the poems. Musashi was a byword in classical verse for a plain with no edge. Fujiwara no Michikata wrote that it had no peak for the moon to set behind. A later parody made it plainer: the moon rises out of the grass and sets into the grass.

The plain is now much of western Tokyo. An old guide to Edo’s famous places already mourned it, cleared four times for new fields. (Poems and guide paraphrased from the Japanese.)

SongJakarta, 1967

Koes Bersaudara, “Di Dalam Bui”

To the So Called “The Guilties” · Mesra · 1967 · the title means “In Prison”

The Koeswoyo brothers of East Java played Beatles-style rock when Sukarno’s government called it ngak ngik ngok, poison for the young. In 1965 three were arrested, and a fourth turned himself in out of solidarity. They were freed on 29 September, the day before the coup attempt, and this song came out of the cell.

Then the provenance turns. In 2023 Yok Koeswoyo, the last surviving member, told Kompas the jailing was “pura-pura”, a pretence, part of the regime’s campaign against what it called “new-style colonialism and imperialism.” It’s the Indonesian neighbour of your Cambodian and Vietnamese likes. I haven’t heard it.

Artifact53 cm tall, about 5,000 years old

“Flame-style” deep pot

Japan · Middle Jōmon period, 3500–2500 BCE · earthenware with cord-marked and incised decoration · Metropolitan Museum 2015.300.258 · public domain

“Flame” is a modern nickname; nobody knows what the rim shows. The type has four large handles, a sawtooth lip, and spirals and S-curves built from coils of clay. They cluster along the Shinano River in Niigata; the first was dug up in 1936.

Some still have burnt food stuck inside, so they were cooking pots, though some think they also served a rite. Your saved folder is full of spirals; here is one in fired clay.

Anecdote421 years ago today

They shall not see who hurts them

On 26 October 1605 an “unknown man” handed Lord Monteagle’s servant a letter for his master. It warned Monteagle, a Catholic, to find an excuse to miss the opening of Parliament:

for though there be no apparance of anni stir, yet I saye they shall receive a terrible blow this parliament and yet they shall not seie who hurts themThe Monteagle letter, National Archives SP 14/216/2

Monteagle took it to the Privy Council; the plotters knew, and went ahead. The king’s men searched the palace and caught a man leaving a room packed with firewood, with barrels of gunpowder hidden in it. He said his name was John Johnson.

His examination that day is the strange part. Asked what government would follow the killing of king, nobles, bishops and judges, he answered that “we were not growne to any determination therein,” and “the people of themselves would decide a head.” Asked why he would kill men of his own religion, he said they had meant to see some safe, “and would have prayed for them.”

Johnson was Guy Fawkes. The Archives’ own note says he is “remembered incorrectly as the main plotter”: he was the explosives man. The Archives still calls the letter anonymous.

The benchRead from the opinion

The employee as captive audience

California Chamber of Commerce v. Bonta

CourtNinth Circuit, No. 25-6874 · Oct. 29, 2026 · published opinion
HoldingPreliminary injunction against California’s SB 399 affirmed. The industry groups are likely to win their First Amendment claim. NLRA preemption not reached.
Vote3–0. No separate opinions.
WhoTallman wrote, joined by Paez and Bennett. Below: Judge Daniel J. Calabretta, E.D. Cal.

SB 399 bars employers from punishing workers who skip meetings “the purpose of which is to communicate the employer’s opinion about religious or political matters,” union organizing included. California called it a rule about conduct. The panel disagreed: you can only tell whether a meeting is covered by asking what the employer said, so the law is content- and speaker-based, and gets strict scrutiny.

The state’s answer was that employees are a captive audience, like riders on a bus or patients outside a clinic. The panel found that the doctrine has never carried this weight:

Neither court has relied on a “captive audience” theory to identify a compelling interest sufficient to uphold content-based restrictions on nongovernmental speech in a nongovernmental setting.

Standing turned on a word: members hold “all-hands” meetings, and the court, citing Merriam-Webster, held the label alone can imply a threat to anyone who stays away. It closed with Erznoznik (1975): “we are inescapably captive audiences for many purposes.”

PaintingAbout 1810–20

Ruins of an Ancient City

John Martin (British, 1789–1854) · oil on paper, mounted on canvas · 95.6 × 118.6 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art 1981.13 · CC0

A framed view, a plunge into a valley, tiny figures. The museum calls the detail “almost archeological” and the drop and the light Romantic. Martin trained in heraldic painting at a Newcastle coachbuilder. Thomas Lawrence called him “the most popular painter of his day”; Ruskin was among his critics.

His brother Jonathan, a preacher “tormented by madness,” set fire to York Minster in 1829. John paid for the defence, and Jonathan was spared the noose on grounds of insanity. An onlooker said the burning cathedral looked like one of John’s pictures.

Poem · GermanGalicia, September 1914

Grodek

Georg Trakl (1887–1914) · first printed in Der Brenner, Innsbruck, 1915 · complete, 17 lines · translation by Claude, unrhymed

Trakl, a pharmacist, was a medical officer at the battle of Gródek, now Horodok in Ukraine, left with about ninety wounded and no anaesthetics for operations. He tried to shoot himself and was stopped. He died of a cocaine overdose in a Kraków military hospital on 3 November 1914, aged 27. Wittgenstein, who had never met him, came to the hospital days too late. Your notes said to read about forty Trakl poems, bilingual. This was probably the last.

Am Abend tönen die herbstlichen Wälder
Von tötlichen Waffen, die goldnen Ebenen
Und blauen Seen, darüber die Sonne
Düster hinrollt; umfängt die Nacht
Sterbende Krieger, die wilde Klage
Ihrer zerbrochenen Münder.

At evening the autumn woods resoundwith deadly weapons, the golden plainsand blue lakes, over which the sunrolls darkly on; night gathers indying warriors, the wild lamentof their broken mouths.

Doch stille sammelt im Weidengrund
Rotes Gewölk, darin ein zürnender Gott wohnt,
Das vergossne Blut sich, mondne Kühle;
Alle Straßen münden in schwarze Verwesung.

But quietly in the willow hollowred cloud, where an angry god lives,the spilled blood gathers, a lunar coolness;all roads run out into black decay.

Unter goldnem Gezweig der Nacht und Sternen
Es schwankt der Schwester Schatten durch den schweigenden Hain,
Zu grüßen die Geister der Helden, die blutenden Häupter;
Und leise tönen im Rohr die dunkeln Flöten des Herbstes.

Under golden boughs of night and starsthe sister’s shadow sways through the silent groveto greet the ghosts of the heroes, the bleeding heads;and softly in the reeds the dark flutes of autumn sound.

O stolzere Trauer! ihr ehernen Altäre,
Die heiße Flamme des Geistes nährt heute ein gewaltiger Schmerz,
Die ungebornen Enkel.

O prouder grief! You altars of bronze,the hot flame of the spirit is fed today by a mighty pain:the unborn grandsons.

The stanza breaks are mine; the poem runs as one block. Trakl’s manuscript has Düstrer (“more darkly”) and tödlichen where the 1915 print has Düster and tötlichen. The grammar of lines 7–9 is knotted on purpose: the subject, the spilled blood, arrives two lines late.

Artifact54.5 cm of hammered gold

Sheet-gold decoration for a sword scabbard

Greek or Scythian · about 340–320 BCE · gold · L. 54.5 cm · Metropolitan Museum 30.11.12 · public domain

The Met can’t say which: its label reads “Greek or Scythian,” and its subject tags are battles, griffins, deer and lions. That uncertainty is the history. By the fourth century BCE Greek goldsmiths were working for rich Scythian patrons, often in Scythian territory. Matching ceremonial weapons, scabbards among them, have turned up in different burial mounds, struck from the same matrix in one workshop. Beside your bronze guardians, it’s the same menace in gold.

SongGrassy Creek, N.C., by way of Pennsylvania

Ola Belle Reed, “My Epitaph”

Ola Belle Reed · Rounder · 1973 by the Registry’s date (Wikipedia gives 1972) · second-to-last track · National Recording Registry, 2018

Trakl writes about the unborn; this song turns down any memorial at all: “Let me go in peace … I don’t want your marble at my head,” and “What we do for each other / Let us do it today.” Reed learned clawhammer banjo as a child in Ashe County and was touring with the North Carolina Ridge Runners by 1936. The Registry essay says she didn’t make recording a priority until 1972, some forty years in, and this album was cut over three days in Gei Zantzinger’s home studio in Devault, Pennsylvania. It sits near Tyler Childers and the Kossoy Sisters in your likes. I haven’t heard it.

California writes “no delegation” into statute

SB 574, signed on Sept. 30 and in force from January, is the first state law on lawyers’ use of generative AI. Every citation in a court filing must be personally verified by a responsible attorney, whatever its origin. Lawyers may not “delegate the practice of law” to generative AI, and arbitrators may not delegate “any part of their decision-making process.” The statute defines neither. I read this in WilmerHale’s analysis; the legislature’s site refused my request.

New York’s bar treats the tool as a non-lawyer

On Saturday, Oct. 31, the New York State Bar Association’s House of Delegates adopted guidelines meant to “ensure that legal decisions are in human hands.” The rules don’t mention AI, so the bar reasons by analogy: a lawyer must make sure a non-lawyer’s work is up to standard, “even if the ‘non-lawyer’ is an AI tool or system.”

“The Moral Gaps in Legal Machines”

Keith Swisher’s paper won this year’s Warren E. Burger Prize from the American Inns of Court, presented at the Supreme Court in October. Machines doing lawyers’ and judges’ work may be more efficient. “These machines will not, however, be ethical or accountable.” Like the Monteagle letter, they leave no one to answer for what they say.
