The Burning of the Houses of Lords and Commons, 16 October 1834

Joseph Mallord William Turner (British, 1775–1851) · 1835 · oil on canvas · 92 × 123.2 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art 1942.647 · CC0

The Palace of Westminster. Cleveland notes that Turner “magnified the height of the flames,” and that the boats at lower right are heading toward them.

What lit it was an accounting system. The Exchequer had kept its records on notched willow tally sticks since medieval times. A 1782 Act moved the records to paper, but only once the last sinecure-holder died, which happened in 1826. That left two cartloads of obsolete sticks. On 16 October 1834 two labourers fed them into the coal furnaces under the House of Lords all day. Dickens, later, on why the sticks weren’t simply given away as firewood:

they never had been useful, and official routine required that they should never be, and so the order went out that they were to be privately and confidentially burnt.Dickens, quoted on Wikipedia

Orange against deep blue is the palette you save most. Turner had it in 1835.

AnecdoteGrand Banks, 18 November 1929

The cables broke in order

At 5:02 p.m. Newfoundland time, 97 years ago today, a magnitude 7.2 earthquake struck about 250 km south of Newfoundland. It set off an undersea slump that, by Natural Resources Canada’s estimate, moved 200 cubic kilometres of material. About two and a half hours later a tsunami reached the Burin Peninsula in three pulses, and at the heads of long narrow bays the water ran as high as 13 metres. Twenty-eight people died.

Nobody outside knew for days. The only telegraph line from the peninsula had, as NRCan puts it, “coincidentally and unfortunately, gone out of service just prior to the earthquake.” The relief ship Meigle reached Burin on the afternoon of 22 November. One man, swept to sea, swam to a floating house and found it was his own. Dr. Mosdell, aboard the Meigle:

Dwelling houses were reduced to a condition reminiscent of wartime description of the effects of heavy shell fire.Daily News, St. John’s, 27 November 1929

Twelve transatlantic cables broke too. The telegraph companies logged when each one went dead: six at the moment of the quake, then six more, deeper and deeper down the slope, over the next 13 hours and 17 minutes. In 1952 Bruce Heezen and Maurice Ewing at Columbia got hold of the 23-year-old records. A fault would have cut them all at once. Heezen and Ewing:

All previously published explanations of these breaks are considered and rejected because they do not adequately explain this sequence.

What cut them was a current of mud and sand running down the slope: the first turbidity current documented in the ocean, found in the companies’ own logs.

아름다운 강산 · Shin Joong Hyun & The Men · 1972 · US pressings of his music on Light in the Attic since 2011

A deep cut for the Cambodian Rocks end of your likes, with a story about refusing authority. In 1972 Park Chung Hee’s office asked Shin to write a song praising the president. Sensitive places and a sleeping car

Novotny v. Moore

CourtU.S. Supreme Court, No. 25-1324 · Nov. 16, 2026 · cert. denied
HoldingNone. The Fourth Circuit’s ruling upholding most of Maryland’s “sensitive place” carry bans stands (Kipke v. Moore, 165 F.4th 194).
WhoThomas, alone, in a statement respecting denial. No noted dissent. I didn’t read the Fourth Circuit opinion.

This is the question Bruen left open. Maryland bars carrying at demonstrations, on transit, at places of amusement, in health facilities, where alcohol is served, and in state parks and forests. Thomas agreed to deny only because the petition was too broad: “each Maryland statute implicates a distinct historical tradition.” Then he widens the view to other states’ lists. New Jersey, he notes, bans guns in at least 25 locations. And:

The lower courts have largely blessed these ahistorical ventures.

His closing line is an invitation: “In an appropriate case, this Court should consider whether particular sensitive-place laws are consistent with the right to public carry.”

Beram v. City of Sedona

CourtNinth Circuit, No. 23-15912 · Nov. 12, 2026 · published
HoldingTitle II of the ADA reaches criminal ordinances. Beram has standing for damages and an injunction. Affirmed in part, reversed in part, remanded.
Vote3–0. Desai wrote, with de Alba and District Judge Chen (by designation). No separate opinions.

Sedona makes sleeping in a vehicle a misdemeanor, punishable by up to six months in jail. Beram has obsessive-compulsive disorder that often keeps her from entering her apartment, so she sleeps in her car, and the city refused to stop enforcing against her at her own complex. Grants Pass closed the Eighth Amendment route; the panel found a statutory one:

Discrimination under Title II may include the failure to provide reasonable accommodation that would allow a person with a disability to comply with a criminal ordinance.

Whether her particular request is reasonable is left for remand. A footnote answers the policy objection: “we are not permitted to legislate from the bench and read into the law statutory exclusions that do not exist.”

Poem · PortugueseLisbon, 1934

Mar Portuguez

Fernando Pessoa (1888–1935) · from Mensagem (1934) · complete, in the original spelling · translation by Claude, unrhymed

The 1929 wave was recorded as far away as Lagos, in Portugal, more than six hours after the quake. This is a poem about what the sea costs, which inverso.pt calls the best-known short poem in the Portuguese language. Cape Bojador, on the African coast, was once held to be the limit of the navigable sea; Gil Eanes sailed past it and came back.

Ó mar salgado, quanto do teu salSão lágrimas de Portugal!Por te cruzarmos, quantas mães choraram,Quantos filhos em vão rezaram!Quantas noivas ficaram por casarPara que fosses nosso, ó mar!

O salt sea, how much of your saltis the tears of Portugal!For our crossing you, how many mothers wept,how many sons prayed in vain!How many brides were left unmarriedso that you might be ours, O sea!

Valeu a pena? Tudo vale a penaSe a alma não é pequena.Quem quere passar além do BojadorTem que passar além da dor.Deus ao mar o perigo e o abysmo deu,Mas nelle é que espelhou o céu.

Was it worth it? Everything is worth itif the soul is not small.Whoever would pass beyond Bojadormust pass beyond the pain.God gave the sea the danger and the abyss,but it was in the sea he mirrored heaven.

Oregon sues OpenAI over children

A day after California’s suit, a separate one on a different theory. Attorney General Dan Rayfield has sued OpenAI in Multnomah County for $2.035 billion under the state’s Unlawful Trade Practices Act, alleging that ChatGPT was built to foster dependence and give “sycophantic” answers, and that its safeguards for children failed. Rayfield: “OpenAI knows that ChatGPT is harming children, and has failed to meaningfully fix their dangerous platform.” These are allegations, from news reports, not the complaint. Maryland takes an AI-citation case to court

Maryland’s Attorney Grievance Commission has petitioned for discipline against a Prince George’s County lawyer accused of filing ChatGPT-invented cases. Investigators reviewed “dozens of ChatGPT logs,” and the petition says he also used ChatGPT to draft his answers to bar counsel’s questions about the fake citations. The Daily Record: until last week, “no disciplinary actions over them had been taken to court and become public record.” These are charges, not findings.

Should the Vatican grade the models?

Matthew Harvey Sanders, whose company Longbeard makes Magisterium AI, told Crux that the Vatican’s new interdicasterial commission on AI could set “an authoritative Catholic benchmark … showing how faithful each model is on the questions that matter most.” His premise: “Infrastructure always carries a worldview. … Whoever owns a system decides what it will and won’t say.” It’s one vendor’s proposal, and Crux notes Longbeard has long sponsored it. On Tuesday the Archbishop of York told the new General Synod the world needs a wisdom “slower and deeper than anything AI can offer.”
