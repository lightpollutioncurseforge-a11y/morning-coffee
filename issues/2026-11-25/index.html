Shipwreck near a Rocky Coast

Thomas Birch (American, born England, 1779–1851) · 1833 · oil on canvas · 51.4 × 76.8 cm · Art Institute of Chicago 2018.363

Philadelphia, and Jacksonian. Birch came over from England in 1794 and helped his father engrave Birch’s Views of Philadelphia. He painted portraits until about 1807, then turned to the sea. He’s best known for War of 1812 sea fights, and one curator calls him “the first American ship portraitist.”

The Art Institute says he “left the narrative unresolved”: you can’t tell whether the small boat makes it to shore. It’s your small-figure, big-world habit, with the colour taken out.

AnecdoteBarfleur, 25 November 1120

The poorest man of all the company

Henry I was crossing home from Normandy with his fleet. Thomas, son of Stephen, offered a gold mark: his father had carried the Conqueror to England “to make war on Harold,” and Thomas had a ship too, the Blanche-Nef. The king had chosen his own ship, but answered: “I entrust to you my sons, William and Richard, whom I love as myself.”

The crew asked the prince for a drink. He gave them three muids of wine, and “no sooner was the wine delivered to them than they had a great drinking bout.” A few got off, seeing it “overcrowded with riotous and headstrong youths.” The priests who came to bless it were driven off “amidst shouts of laughter.” Thomas raced to overtake the fleet and struck a rock. “Two planks having been shattered by the crash, the ship, alas! filled and went down.”

Two men held on to the yard. Thomas surfaced and called out: “What has become of the king’s son?” Told the prince was drowned, he said, “it is misery for me to live any longer,” and let go. In the frost the young noble, Geoffrey de l’Aigle, let go too. The survivor was a butcher of Rouen:

Berold, however, who was the poorest man of all the company, and wore a sheep-skin dress, was the only one among so many who survived till the dawn of another day.Orderic Vitalis, tr. Thomas Forester (1856)

Nobody dared tell the king for a day, until a boy was sent to weep at his feet. Henry “instantly fell to the ground.” Berold lived twenty more years.

Orderic adds a moon in her nineteenth day, lighting the water. His French editor, quoted by Forester, calls it “entirely apocryphal”: that night was so near the new moon that it was invisible for most of it. He also got the year wrong. Nobody doubts the sheepskin.

PaintingAn album leaf, 29 cm tall

Su Wu the Shepherd

Min Zhen (Chinese, 1730–after 1788) · 1788 · album leaf, ink on paper · 29 × 18.4 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art 1985.71.10

Another man who kept warm in sheep’s wool. In about 100 BCE the Han envoy Su Wu was detained by the Xiongnu. Starved in a cellar, he ate snow and the wool of his coat. Then he was sent to Lake Baikal to herd male sheep and told he could go home when they bore young. He kept the emperor’s staff of office until all its decorative hairs fell off.

He came home after nineteen years by a lie. When the Xiongnu claimed he was dead, Han envoys said the emperor had shot a goose with a letter from Su Wu tied to its foot. Min Zhen, an orphan known as an eccentric, paints the goose.

A fact nobody had published, and a trust with nobody in it

United States ex rel. Berkley v. Ocean State, LLC

CourtFirst Circuit, No. 25-2033 · Nov. 24, 2026 · published
HoldingThe False Claims Act’s public disclosure bar is not jurisdictional. It’s an affirmative defense the defendants must prove, and here they didn’t. Summary judgment vacated, case remanded.
Vote3–0. Aframe wrote for Gelpí and Rikelman. No separate opinions.

Berkley was landlord to Ocean State, a private-equity-owned company that took a PPP loan and then warned it might miss the rent. He asked the CEO why the fund didn’t pay and heard, “that’s not how it works.” He sued, saying the companies falsely certified they needed the loans. The district court ruled the loan data and ownership were already public. The panel says the public record showed the certification but not the alleged truth behind it, that the fund’s money was available. The defendants called that a mere assumption:

But an assumption can be an assertion of fact -- just one that is unsupported by evidence.

Whether he can prove it is still open.

United States v. Rhodes

CourtSixth Circuit, No. 25-1842 · Nov. 20, 2026 · published
HoldingAffirmed. Rhodes owes $558,352.96 plus interest, and his Harsens Island home is sold. The “common-law trust” that held it was no trust under Michigan law, because it had no definite beneficiary.
Vote3–0. Murphy wrote for Stranch and Larsen. No separate opinions.

A sign-maker bought “pure trust organizations” from a promoter, put his businesses and house into them, and filed no return after 1997. The pitch, quoted by the court: “if an IRS agent ever visits you at your work place, you can honestly tell him, ‘I just work here, go talk to the owner.’” He named the house trust after a date that mattered to him, July Thirtyfirst.

The trust papers gave the certificate holders only a claim against the trustee. Murphy:

Yet the ability to enforce a contract does not make a contracting party a beneficiary of a trust. The contrary rule would turn all contracts into trusts.

Poem · ItalianRecanati, 1819

L’infinito

Giacomo Leopardi (1798–1837) · complete, 15 lines · text from the Moroncini edition on Italian Wikisource · translation by Claude, unrhymed

A hedge blocks the view, so the poet builds the infinite behind it in his head. Wikipedia dates it “probably” to autumn 1819, when Leopardi was about 21 and longed to leave Recanati. It ends with a shipwreck: naufragar. Today it’s the only sweet one.

Sempre caro mi fu quest’ermo colleE questa siepe, che da tanta parteDell’ultimo orizzonte il guardo esclude.Ma sedendo e mirando, interminatiSpazi di là da quella, e sovrumaniSilenzi, e profondissima quieteIo nel pensier mi fingo; ove per pocoIl cor non si spaura.

This lonely hill was always dear to me,and this hedge, that shuts the gaze awayfrom so much of the farthest horizon.But sitting here and looking, I inventin thought the endless spaces past it,more-than-human silences,and the deepest quiet, where the heartis very nearly frightened.

E come il ventoOdo stormir tra queste piante, io quelloInfinito silenzio a questa voceVo comparando: e mi sovvien l’eterno,E le morte stagioni, e la presenteE viva, e il suon di lei. Così tra questaImmensità s’annega il pensier mio:E il naufragar m’è dolce in questo mare.

And as I hearthe wind rustle through these trees, I gocomparing that infinite silenceto this voice: and I remember the eternal,and the dead seasons, and the present one,alive, and the sound of it. So in thisimmensity my thought is drowned,and shipwreck is sweet to me in this sea.

Alan Stivell, “Ys”

Renaissance de la harpe celtique · Fontana, 1972 · side A, track 1 · “Variations on folk themes,” traditional, arranged by Stivell

A drowned city to go with a drowned ship. In most versions of the legend Ys lay in Douarnenez Bay, in Brittany, and “was engulfed by a flood as punishment for its sins.” Forester says the clergy passed the same verdict on the Blanche-Nef. Debussy’s “The Engulfed Cathedral” uses the same legend. Next to your Delphic Hymn, it’s another old tune rebuilt.

An Effect of the Sun, Normandy

Gustave Le Gray (French, 1820–1884) · c. 1856 · albumen print from wet collodion negative · 32 × 41.8 cm · Cleveland Museum of Art 1987.54

The same Channel, seven centuries on. Emulsions then couldn’t expose sea and sky together, so for many seascapes Le Gray printed two negatives on one sheet, water from one and sky from another. Cleveland says the usual join “is not visible in this skillfully executed print.” Orderic invented his moon; Le Gray borrowed his sky, as craft rather than fable.

AIMachines and authority

The Tenth Circuit makes a human sign for every citation

The Tenth Circuit has adopted an amended Rule 46.5, effective 1 January, reportedly the first federal appellate rule aimed at AI. The signer certifies that a human “independently verified the existence of all cited authorities.” September’s proposal covered AI-assisted documents. The adopted text reportedly applies to every filing: the certifications “have the same force and effect regardless of whether the brief, motion or paper was prepared” with AI. New sanctions include waiver or striking the document. I’m relying on reports, because the court’s website didn’t answer.

“You can’t put a machine in jail”

Politico reports that the U.S. bishops’ AI task force is planning a national lobbying push in Congress and state legislatures. Its asks include clear human accountability when an autonomous system causes harm. Its chair, Bishop Oscar Cantú, put the case for that in two sentences: “You can’t put a machine in jail. You can’t try them.” I read this in La Voce di New York’s write-up, not the Politico original.

An editor asks the models to review him

Crux’s editor, Charles Collins, gave his own column to several chatbots and found they flattered him. One reply flagged a “‘Sycophancy’ Trap.” His conclusion: “It is a difficult task when the computers are designed to seem so ‘human.’” The task force’s line, he notes, is “neither naïve enthusiasm nor unfounded fear.”
