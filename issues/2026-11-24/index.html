AnecdoteAmsterdam, 27 July 1656

Cursed be he by day

When Spinoza was 23, the lay leaders of the Talmud Torah congregation read a writ of herem against him in front of the ark. It names no offence. It speaks of “abominable heresies which he practiced and taught” and “monstrous deeds,” and then piles up curses:

Cursed be he by day and cursed be he by night; cursed be he when he lies down and cursed be he when he rises up. Cursed be he when he goes out and cursed be he when he comes in.Translation modified from Asa Kasher and Shlomo Biderman, as printed by Steven Nadler

It closes by forbidding anyone to “stay with him under the same roof nor [come] within four cubits in his vicinity; nor shall he read any treatise composed or written by him.” He hadn’t written one yet.

The community banned about forty people between 1622 and 1683, and Steven Nadler says none of the other bans “even approaches the wrath and vitriol” of this one. His explanation is partly political. These were former conversos, not citizens, “worried about how they were perceived by their Dutch hosts,” who took the soul’s immortality very seriously. The young Spinoza was reportedly saying the soul dies with the body.

He wasn’t in the synagogue. His reported answer was: “All the better; they do not force me to do anything that I would not have done of my own accord if I did not dread scandal.”

He went on grinding lenses, and his signet ring read Caute, “Caution.” He died at 44, probably of tuberculosis, “possibly complicated by silicosis brought on by grinding glass lenses.” The ban still stands. In 2015 the congregation’s rabbi ruled that it should hold, on the grounds that he had no greater wisdom than his predecessors.

The benchRead from the opinions

A rescue with no witnesses, and a bet on an old man’s life

Voegeli v. Cao

CourtD.C. Circuit, No. 25-5257 · decided Nov. 20, 2026
HoldingAffirmed. The Navy didn’t act arbitrarily by refusing its Navy and Marine Corps Medal to a man who saved a life, because his nomination lacked two eyewitness statements.
Vote3–0 in the result. Walker wrote for the court, with Millett on the panel. Edwards concurred in the judgment only, and the file I read has no separate opinion from him.

In the summer of 1992, Nicholas Voegeli was a Navy Reserve lieutenant and a sailing instructor on Lake Mendota. When a student capsized and began to drown, he swam two hundred feet through rough water and kept him afloat until a motorboat came. The Coast Guard took sworn statements from the student and the boat’s operator and gave him a lesser award. In 2018 he sought the Navy medal, which requires two eyewitness statements. The originals were gone. His own account can’t count, and his old executive officer could only swear they had once existed.

Walker ends:

With skill and courage, Nicholas Voegeli saved another man’s life. But for reasons largely outside his control, Voegeli’s nomination for the Navy and Marine Corps Medal did not include the type of evidence required for his heroism to be recognized with that award.

Estate of Joseph H. Daher v. LSH Co.

CourtNinth Circuit, No. 24-5398 · filed Nov. 23, 2026
HoldingReversed. A California court can hear the estate’s suit to recover a $5 million death benefit from a Luxembourg fund.
Vote3–0. Sung wrote, with McKeown and Friedland.

LSH has no employees. In the opinion’s words, its “sole purpose is to hold investments in life insurance policies issued by U.S. life insurers primarily (if not exclusively) on the lives of U.S. senior citizens.” In 2012 it bought the policy on Joseph Daher, a Californian in his mid-eighties. Its contractors checked with his family every quarter about his health and collected all his medical records. After he died in December 2017, they mailed California a request for his death certificate and collected. Delaware, whose law governs the policy, calls such stranger-originated policies illegal contracts “wagering on human life,” and lets the estate claw back the money.

LSH said its California contacts were by-products of a nationwide portfolio. The panel rejected the argument that “because a nationwide company is everywhere, it is jurisdictionally nowhere except in its principal place of business and state of incorporation,” and added: “Express aiming does not require differential targeting.”

Poem · HebrewAl-Andalus, early 12th c.

My heart is in the East

Judah Halevi (Toledo or Tudela, c. 1075–1141) · complete, three long lines · text from Hebrew Wikisource · translation by Claude, unrhymed

Five centuries before the herem, a Sephardic physician in Spain had the opposite trouble: nobody was driving him out, and he wanted to leave. In the Kuzari he argued, in Wikipedia’s summary, for “accessing God through tradition and devotion rather than philosophical speculation,” which is the road Spinoza turned down. Late in life Halevi went. He reached Alexandria on 8 September 1140, sailed on 14 May 1141, and died within months, perhaps in Egypt and perhaps in Palestine. The story that a horseman trampled him at Jerusalem’s gates first turns up about 450 years later. All three long lines end on the same sound, -rav, which the first half-line has already struck: West, sweet, Arabia and ruined all rhyme.

לִבִּי בְמִזְרָח וְאָנֹכִי בְּסוֹף מַעֲרָבאֵיךְ אֶטְעֲמָה אֵת אֲשֶׁר אֹכַל וְאֵיךְ יֶעֱרָב

My heart is in the East, and I am at the far end of the West.How can I taste what I eat? How could it be sweet?

אֵיכָה אֲשַׁלֵּם נְדָרַי וָאֱסָרַי, בְּעוֹדצִיּוֹן בְּחֶבֶל אֱדוֹם וַאֲנִי בְּכֶבֶל עֲרָב

How can I pay my vows and my bonds, whileZion lies in the cords of Edom, and I in the chains of Arabia?

יֵקַל בְּעֵינַי עֲזֹב כָּל טוּב סְפָרַד, כְּמוֹיֵקַר בְּעֵינַי רְאוֹת עַפְרוֹת דְּבִיר נֶחֱרָב.

It would be easy in my eyes to leave all the good of Spain,as it is precious in my eyes to see the dust of the ruined sanctuary.

Song · 4:02Recorded December 1966

Duke Ellington, “Isfahan”

Far East Suite · released 1967 · Ellington and Billy Strayhorn · Johnny Hodges, alto saxophone

The heart-in-the-East record, from the Lateef and Brubeck end of your likes. It grew from the orchestra’s 1963 tour through Damascus, Kabul, Tehran, Isfahan and Baghdad. The band reached Ankara, but Kennedy was killed the day before its concert, and the rest of the tour was cancelled. “Isfahan” had been written months before the trip, under the title “Elf.” Strayhorn died in May 1967. Cook and Morton say the album “really should have been The Near East Suite,” and call this track “arguably the most beautiful item in Ellington’s and Strayhorn’s entire output.” I haven’t heard it.

Song · 2:19Ladino

The Western Wind, “Ocho kandelikas”

Milken Archive of Jewish Music, Vol. 4: Cycle of Life in Synagogue and Home · the archive dates the recording 1991, New York · Flory Jagoda’s song

Spinoza’s family came out of Iberia north, to Amsterdam. Flory Jagoda’s came east, to the Ottoman Balkans, and kept its Spanish. Born in Sarajevo in 1923, she escaped at 16 to Split on false papers, playing her accordion the whole way; nobody asked for her ticket. Forty-two of her relatives ended in one mass grave. To the NEA she said: “Some people write books to remember. I write songs to remember.”

This is her Hanukkah song: eight candles, and little cakes “with almonds and honey,” served, the archive says, at the matchmaking parties of her childhood. The sources disagree on its origin. Wikipedia says she wrote it in 1983, and the archive calls it her reconstruction of a traditional tune. This is a choral version, and I haven’t heard it.

AIWho checks, and who vouches

A UK Supreme Court judge says machines may have to decide small claims

Lord Sales, the court’s deputy president, is quoted in Monday’s Telegraph saying that AI-written claims are producing a “tsunami,” and “AI may itself have to be enlisted by the courts to become part of the solution.” For low-value disputes, “we may eventually accept forms of automated determination, particularly where parties agree.” His warning points the other way: “If judges cease to research, analyse and write because machines increasingly perform those functions, critical faculties will atrophy.” And: “The ‘human in the loop’ can gradually become the human rubber stamp.”
