Poem · Arabical-Andalus, 11th–12th c.

The Mountain

Ibn Khafaja (Alzira, near Valencia, 1058–1138/9) · excerpt, 8 couplets · translation by Claude, unrhymed

A traveller alone at night stops to listen to a mountain. Al-Buhturi, in your translations folder, stood in front of ruined stone and did the talking. Here the stone talks back. The red tassels are the line to keep.

وأرعن طماح الذؤابة باذخيطاول أعنان السماء بغارب

A jutting crag, its forelock straining up,its withers reaching for the rim of the sky,

يسد مهب الريح عن كل وجهةويزحم ليلا شهبه بالمناكب

dams the wind from every quarter,and shoulders through the night against its stars.

وقور على ظهر الفلاة كأنهطوال الليالي مفكر في العواقب

Grave on the desert’s back, as if it spentthe long nights thinking how things end.

يلوث عليه الغيم سود عمائملها من وميض البرق حمر ذوائب

The cloud winds black turbans round it;the lightning’s flash gives them red tassels.

أصخت إليه وهو أخرس صامتفحدثني ليل السرى بالعجائب

I bent my ear to it, mute and silent,and through that night’s march it told me wonders.

وكم مر بي من مدلج ومؤوبوقال بظلي من مطي وراكب

How many have passed me, by night and by day,and rested at noon in my shade, mount and rider.

فما كان إلا أن طوتهم يد الردىوطارت بهم ريح النوى والنوائب

Then nothing: death’s hand folded them away,and the wind of parting and disaster flew off with them.

· · · the mountain mourns its companions, and the poet is consoled · · ·

وقلت وقد نكبت عنه لطيةسلام فإنا من مقيم وذاهب

And I said, turning off toward my road:Peace. Of the two of us, one stays and one goes.

Song · 11:10Hard bop, with oud

Ahmed Abdul-Malik, “Ya Annas”

Jazz Sahara · Riverside · recorded October 1958, New York · Johnny Griffin, saxophone; Naim Karacand, violin

From the Yusef Lateef end of your likes, with a provenance puzzle. Abdul-Malik was Thelonious Monk’s bassist and doubled on oud, and he said his father came from Sudan. He was born Jonathan Tim Jr. in Brooklyn, and took his new name after converting to Islam at school. The historian Robin Kelley found his father’s death certificate, which gives St. Vincent in the Caribbean. The ancestry looks invented.

AnecdoteWarwick and Westminster, 1817–19

Ready to defend the same with my body

In May 1817 Mary Ashford, about 20, was found drowned in a pit near Birmingham. A Warwick jury took about six minutes to acquit Abraham Thornton. Her brother William then reached for a medieval device, the private appeal of murder, which could put Thornton on trial again.

On 17 November, in King’s Bench, Thornton pleaded: “Not guilty; and I am ready to defend the same with my body.” He pulled on one leather gauntlet and threw down the other. Ashford’s counsel objected that he shouldn’t be allowed to add the brother to his victims. Lord Ellenborough answered:

It is the law of England, Mr Clarke; we must not call it murder.

Five months later all four judges ruled for Thornton. Ellenborough again: “The general law of this land is in favour of the wager of battle.” William Ashford declined to fight, and Thornton left by a side door.

Parliament moved in June 1819. The Lords took all three readings in a single night, and the Act calls battle “a mode of trial unfit to be used.” It’s your Forms of Action, with a glove in it.

Three October orders the Court wrote about

FCC v. Brown

CourtU.S. Supreme Court, No. 26A476 · Oct. 14, 2026 · per curiam
HoldingStay granted. The Fourth Circuit’s mandamus, giving the FCC 48 hours to decide a pending application, was “inappropriate under the circumstances.”
VoteUnsigned. Sotomayor and Jackson would deny, without an opinion. Others not stated.
BelowFourth Circuit, “the same” divided panel. This opinion doesn’t name the judges, and I didn’t read their order.

The dispute is who gets the cheap “lowest unit charge” for campaign ads. In September the Court stayed this panel’s ruling for likely lack of jurisdiction, since the application was still at the agency. A month later the panel told the FCC to finish in two days. The Act requires a decision but sets no deadline, so timing is the agency’s. The Court was blunt, for a stay order:

Our system of separation of powers does not allow the Judiciary to meddle in “the workings of a coordinate branch of the Government” in this manner.

Scanlan v. Coalition for Open Democracy

CourtU.S. Supreme Court, No. 26A512 · Oct. 22, 2026 · unsigned order
HoldingStay granted of a New Hampshire district court’s Oct. 2 and 5 injunctions, pending the First Circuit.
VoteJackson dissenting, the only noted dissent. It isn’t a full head count.

New Hampshire votes under this today. The district court left the statute alone and told the secretary of state to stop describing the voter-ID rules in ways it found misleading. The Court stayed that in a few sentences: lower courts “should ordinarily not alter the election rules on the eve of an election.” Does an order about what an official says alter a rule? Jackson, on Purcell, says it needn’t:

When a federal court determines that material misrepresentations are being made about a State’s election rules, an injunction to prohibit that conduct does not contribute to public confusion, it prevents that very problem.

O’Donnell v. City of Chicago

CourtU.S. Supreme Court, No. 25-1098 · Oct. 19, 2026 · cert. denied
HoldingNone. The Seventh Circuit’s ruling for the city stands, 163 F.4th 411 (2025).
WhoStatement by Sotomayor, joined by Gorsuch, agreeing with the denial for now. The statement doesn’t name the panel.

The pairing is the story. Chicago can keep a car over as few as two unpaid tickets. One petitioner’s went for $273 in scrap over about $524 in tickets. The Seventh Circuit called it police power. Sotomayor sees debt collection, which would put it under Tyler v. Hennepin County, and warns against “the talismanic invocation of the State’s ‘law enforcement authority.’”

Grotto in an iceberg

Herbert Ponting · Terra Nova Expedition · January 1911 · green-toned carbon print · public domain

A framed view, a tiny ship, enormous cold. The ship is the Terra Nova. Ponting was the first professional photographer on an Antarctic expedition, and that same season killer whales broke up the floe under him. After Scott’s death, in Wikipedia’s phrase, his work became “a memorial to Scott and his companions rather than a celebration.”

Upright bell (nao)

China · Shang dynasty · 13th century BCE · bronze · Metropolitan Museum 49.136.10

This is your bronze mask, as an instrument. The face is the taotie common on Shang music-making bronzes, and nao usually came in sets of three or five. Christie’s has these standing on the hollow shank, struck with a mallet, and a study of nao from south China says one bell can give two tones.
