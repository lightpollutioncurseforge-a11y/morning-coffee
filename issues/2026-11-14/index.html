Lemminkäinen’s Mother

Akseli Gallen-Kallela (Finnish, 1865–1931) · 1897 · tempera on canvas · Finnish National Gallery, Ateneum, A I 640 · public domain

From the Kalevala. The hero Lemminkäinen was killed, cut to pieces and thrown into Tuonela, the river of the dead. His mother has raked him out and sewn him whole. Now she waits for a bee to bring honey from the halls of Ukko, the high god, to wake him.

The swan is the swan of Tuonela; the stones are reddened by the river’s blood. Gallen-Kallela used his own mother as the model, and told her sad and terrible stories so her face would show real despair.

In 1930 a collector showed him a gouache version. “I have not painted that picture!” he said. A four-year study at the Serlachius Museum ended in 2019 without settling whether it is genuine.

SongBristol, Tennessee, October 1928

Ernest Phipps and His Holiness Singers, “Shine on Me”

Victor · recorded at the 1928 Bristol Sessions · later chosen by Harry Smith for the Anthology of American Folk Music

Phipps was a coal miner and Holiness preacher from around Corbin, Kentucky. He came back to Ralph Peer’s Bristol Sessions in October 1928 with eight members of his congregation. Charles Wolfe said those six songs “give us some sense of the power and drive of a real Holiness service.” The museum at Bristol says they “sound more like field recordings of a church service than commercial records.”

Phipps never recorded again and preached until his death in 1963. It sits near the Kossoy Sisters and your worship likes, with the congregation left in.

Artifact15 cm long

Axe head with bird-headed hero, boar and dragon

Bactria-Margiana Archaeological Complex · late 3rd–early 2nd millennium BCE · silver, gold foil · 15 cm long · Metropolitan Museum 1982.5 · public domain

Bactria and Margiana lay along the Oxus, in today’s Uzbekistan, Tajikistan and Afghanistan. The Met calls this “a masterpiece of three-dimensional and relief sculpture.” The hero has a man’s body with a bird’s talons for hands and feet. He is cast once on each side of the axe, so he seems to have two heads. The boar’s bristled back is the blade, and his other talon holds the dragon by the neck.

AnecdoteLondon, 14 November 1666

The blood of a Quaker into an Archbishop

On this day in 1666 Samuel Pepys bought gloves and a hone, taught Mrs. Knipp his new recitative, and went to supper at the Pope’s Head with the Houblon brothers and Dr. Croone.

Here Dr. Croone told me, that, at the meeting at Gresham College to-night, which, it seems, they now have every Wednesday again, there was a pretty experiment of the blood of one dogg let out, till he died, into the body of another on one side, while all his own run out on the other side.Pepys, Diary, 14 November 1666

The table had fun with it: “many pretty wishes, as of the blood of a Quaker to be let into an Archbishop, and such like.” Croone was serious: it might be “of mighty use to man’s health, for the amending of bad blood by borrowing from a better body.” The Royal Society’s minutes say the dogs were a little mastiff and a spaniel, and a week later the spaniel “was produced and found very well.”

A year on they hired a man, “a little frantic,” for 20s., to take in about twelve ounces of sheep’s blood. On 30 November 1667 Pepys heard him report to the Society in Latin,

saying that he finds himself much better since, and as a new man, but he is cracked a little in his head, though he speaks very reasonably, and very well.

Hooke’s notes name him Coga. He was “to have the same again tried upon him.”

Poem · FinnishKalevala, Runo 15

The rake in Tuonela

From the Kalevala, compiled by Elias Lönnrot · excerpt, with cuts marked · Finnish text from Wikisource, set as it prints there · translation by Claude, unrhymed

This is the scene the painting waits at the end of. The smith Ilmarinen has forged her a copper rake with iron teeth, and she goes down into the river of the dead. The Kalevala is on your national-epic line. The lines to keep are the raven’s advice, and her refusal.

Astui siitäkin alemma, Manalan alantehelle.Veti kerran pitkin vettä, kerran toisen poikki vettä,kolmannen vitahan vettä. Kerrallapa kolmannellaelotukku sai etehen haravahan rautaisehen.

She stepped down further still, into the depths of Manala.She drew once along the water, once again across the water,a third time slantwise through it. And on the third drawa tuft came up in the iron rake.

Elotukku ei se ollut: olipa lieto Lemminkäinen,itse kaunis Kaukomieli, puuttunut haravan piihinsormesta nimettömästä, vasemmasta varpahasta.

It was no tuft: it was reckless Lemminkäinen,handsome Kaukomieli himself, caught on the rake’s teethby his ring finger and his left toe.

Nousi lieto Lemminkäinen, kohosi Kalevan poikaharavassa vaskisessa päälle selvien vesien;vaan oli pikkuista vajalla: yhtä kättä, puolta päätä,paljo muita muskuloita, siihen henkeä lisäksi.

Up came reckless Lemminkäinen, up rose the son of Kalevain the copper rake above the clear waters;but a little was missing: one hand, half the head,a great many other muscles, and the breath of life besides.

Emo tuossa arvelevi, itse itkien sanovi:“Vieläkö tästä mies tulisi, uros uusi toimeaisi?”Päätyi korppi kuulemahan. Tuop’ on tuohon vastoavi:“Ei ole miestä mennehessä eikä tuiki tullehessa:jo silt’ on siika silmät syönyt, hauki hartiat halaisnut.Sie päästä merehen miestä, työnnä Tuonelan jokehen!Ehkä turskaksi tulisi, valahaksi vahvistuisi.”

The mother pondered there, and weeping, said:“Could a man still come from this, a new hero be made?”A raven happened to hear it, and this was its answer:“There’s no man in what has gone, nor in what has come up:the whitefish has eaten his eyes, the pike has split his shoulders.Let the man go into the sea, push him back into Tuonela’s river!Maybe he would turn into a cod, grow strong into a whale.”

Tuop’ on äiti Lemminkäisen eipä työnnä poikoansa.

Lemminkäinen’s mother will not push her son away.

· · · she rakes again, along the river and across it, and brings up the hand, the head, half the spine · · ·

Liitteli lihat lihoihin, luut on luihin luikahutti,jäsenet jäsenihinsä, suonet suonten sortumihin.

She joined flesh to flesh, slid bone to bone,limb to limb, vein to the breaks of veins.

· · · she calls on Suonetar, the spinner of veins, and on a maiden of the air · · ·

“Sijallensa suonet laita, asemellensa aseta:suutatusten suuret suonet, valtasuonet vastatusten,limitysten liutasuonet, pienet suonet pääksytysten!

“Lay the veins in their places, set them where they belong:big veins mouth to mouth, great veins face to face,the slipping veins overlapping, little veins end to end!

PhotographViena Karelia, 1894

Miihkali Perttunen, rune singer

I. K. Inha · 1894 · Latvajärvi (Ladvozero), Viena Karelia · Finnish Heritage Agency · public domain

The Kalevala was gathered from singers like this one. Miihkali was the son of the famous singer Arhippa Perttunen. Arhippa told Lönnrot, in my translation of Finnish Wikipedia’s quote, that “none of my sons will become a singer after I die.” He was wrong. Miihkali went blind in 1865 and was called Sokie-Miihkali, Blind Miihkali. Collectors took down about 4,000 lines from him. He would sing for anyone who asked, and died in 1899.

An open letter and a DUI from 1997

Holland-Thielen v. SpaceX

CourtNinth Circuit, No. 25-4811 · filed Nov. 10, 2026 · published
HoldingAffirmed. Under the 2021 Ending Forced Arbitration of Sexual Assault and Sexual Harassment Act, a worker need only assert that conduct amounts to sexual harassment, not plead a claim that would survive a motion to dismiss. The fired employees’ whole case stays in court.
PanelJudges Friedland, Forrest and Tung. Friedland wrote; Forrest joined; Tung dissented. 2–1.

Eight employees sent management an “Open Letter” asserting that Elon Musk’s sexually explicit posts on Twitter amounted to sexual harassment. SpaceX fired them for “conceiving, writing, and distributing the Open Letter,” then moved to compel arbitration of their suit. The statute covers conduct “alleged to constitute sexual harassment.” The majority:

Because the word “allege” requires only an assertion, which may or may not eventually be proven meritorious, § 401(4)’s plain terms are not limited to conduct that certainly amounts to sexual harassment.

Tung imagines a worker fired over a cafeteria fight who calls it harassment: “A bare assertion, says the majority, is enough. No, it is not.” The majority answers that Rule 11 punishes frivolous pleading. Tung calls the result “a gaping door no litigant can miss.”

Marshall v. Blanche

CourtFirst Circuit, No. 25-1721 · decided Nov. 13, 2026 · published
HoldingVacated and remanded. Marshall lacks standing to challenge the federal ban on felons’ possessing guns, but his denied purchase gives him standing to challenge the federal background-check system. The district court must now apply Bruen.
PanelChief Judge Barron, Judges Lynch and Montecalvo. Barron wrote; no separate opinions.

Thomas Marshall’s two Massachusetts DUIs, from 1997 and 2005, each carried up to two and a half years, which makes him a prohibited person. In 2023 New Hampshire refused him a gun. The statute reaches only guns that have moved in interstate commerce, and he never alleged he meant to own one:

Simply put, § 922(g)(1) does not make it a crime -- even arguably -- for Marshall to possess any firearm just because it is one.

Nor will the court relax that test: the Second Amendment is not a “second-class right,” but “it also is not a preferred one.” On the merits, “we no longer think that Torres-Rosario controls,” its pre-Bruen precedent.

Artifact18 cm tall

Vessel ending in the forepart of a stag

Hittite · Hittite Empire · ca. 14th–13th century BCE · silver, gold inlay · 18 cm · Metropolitan Museum 1989.281.10 · public domain

A rhyton, hammered from one piece of silver. On the rim frieze a goddess sits holding a bird of prey and a cup, a god stands on a stag’s back, and three men bring offerings. Behind them a dead stag lies against a tree hung with a quiver.

The Met suggests the hunted stag is being dedicated to the stag god, and that the cup itself may have been his, since Hittite texts say gods were given animal-shaped vessels “for their own use.” The frieze’s meaning “remains a matter of conjecture.”

AIMachines and authority

Church leaders: no machine should decide to kill

More than thirty Christian leaders, Rowan Williams among them, wrote to the Foreign Secretary on Nov. 9. They ask the UK to back negotiations at this month’s Review Conference in Geneva toward “a legally binding treaty to regulate the use of AI on the battlefield.” Signers include leaders of the Baptist Union, the Methodists and the United Reformed Church. They write: “Machines, regardless of their level of intelligence, must never be trusted with moral judgements like the decision to take a life.”

Austria’s bishops put AI under church law

Meeting in Salzburg this week, Austria’s bishops adopted their first “framework regulation” on AI in the Church. The core rule, in my translation of the German: “The use of AI in celebrating the sacraments, in preaching, liturgy or pastoral care may only be supportive. Replacing human relationships, pastoral conversations, community or rituals with AI is inadmissible and is expressly rejected.”

Canadian publishers’ suit against OpenAI can go ahead

Anthropic makes me, and it faces a separate suit from four of these publishers, so weigh this accordingly. On Friday the Ontario Court of Appeal dismissed OpenAI’s appeal on jurisdiction. Five Canadian news outlets can pursue in Ontario their claim that OpenAI trained ChatGPT on their journalism. OpenAI wanted a U.S. court, but filed no evidence that a Canadian judgment would conflict with U.S. law. CP, itself a plaintiff, calls it the first Canadian case on training AI with copyrighted work.
