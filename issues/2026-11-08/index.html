Gothic Windows in the Ruins of the Monastery at Oybin

Carl Gustav Carus (German, 1789–1869) · ca. 1828 · oil on canvas · 43.2 × 33.7 cm · Metropolitan Museum 2007.192 · public domain

Carus was a doctor. From 1814 he was professor of obstetrics and ran the maternity clinic in Dresden, and from 1814 to 1817 he taught himself oil painting under Caspar David Friedrich. He was a friend of Goethe. He had a name for the landscape painting he wanted: Erdlebenbildkunst, pictures of the life of the earth.

The church is on the Oybin, a sandstone hill in southeast Saxony. Emperor Charles IV began it in 1366, with help from Peter Parler’s Prague cathedral workshop, and founded a Celestine monastery there in 1369. The Reformation dissolved the monastery. In 1577 lightning struck the church and it burned. Later the people of the valley used the castle as a quarry. Romantic painters rediscovered the place, and from 1829, a year or so after this picture, the site was cleared of rubble.

SongCassette, 1978

Hailu Mergia & the Dhalak Band, “Anchin Kfu Ayinkash”

Wede Harer Guzo (“Journey to Harar”) · cassette, 1978 · reissued 2016 by Awesome Tapes From Africa

Under the Derg, Hailu said, “When you sing or write lyrics you have to support the government, and if you don’t do that then you have a problem.” One way round that was to leave out the words: his Walias Band’s Tche Belew (1977) was one of only three instrumental albums of Addis Ababa’s golden age. This side-project cassette is described as jazz-infused and dominated by improvisation. I haven’t heard it, and the page doesn’t say whether this track has words.

Then your kind of provenance. He stayed in the US after a 1980s tour and from 1998 drove a taxi out of Dulles: “After I drop my customer, I grab my keyboard from the trunk and sit in the car and practice.” A label owner found one of his cassettes in a bin; reissues began in 2013.

Artifact19 cm wide

Harpoon counterweight

Old Bering Sea III · Bering Strait region · 300–500 CE · walrus ivory · 6.4 × 19.1 × 3.8 cm · Metropolitan Museum 2019.456.17 · public domain

The name “winged object” goes back to George Byron Gordon, who visited the Bering Strait in 1905 and decided one was the head of a ceremonial staff, its wings a whale’s tail. The Sainsbury Centre, which holds a similar piece, says it’s now well established that they’re harpoon counterweights and stabilisers. They were carved, it says, “with great care, beauty, and spiritual significance.”

You saved a walrus-ivory chess knight. This is the same tusk, worked a world away: dotted circles and spirals cut into a piece of hunting gear.

AnecdoteWürzburg, 8 November 1895

Behind a book of about 1000 pages

Late on Friday afternoon, 131 years ago today, Wilhelm Röntgen wrapped a Crookes–Hittorf tube in black cardboard and darkened his lab to make sure the cover was light-tight. It was. Then he noticed a faint shimmer on a bench a few feet away. He struck a match. It was the barium platinocyanide screen he had meant to use next.

For weeks he ate and slept in the lab. His first paper, published on 28 December, reads like a man emptying his desk in front of the beam:

hinter einem eingebundenen Buch von ca. 1000 Seiten sah ich den Fluorescenzschirm noch deutlich leuchten; die Druckerschwärze bietet kein merkliches Hinderniss.“behind a bound book of about 1000 pages I still saw the screen clearly glow; the printer’s ink is no noticeable obstacle.” The screen also glowed behind a double pack of whist cards, and a single card barely showed. Translations by Claude.

Then: “Hält man die Hand zwischen den Entladungsapparat und den Schirm, so sieht man die dunkleren Schatten der Handknochen in dem nur wenig dunklen Schattenbild der Hand.” Hold your hand between the tube and the screen, and you see the darker shadows of its bones inside the faint shadow of the hand. About six weeks in, he photographed his wife Anna Bertha’s hand. Wikipedia gives her reply: “I have seen my death!” He refused to patent any of it.

Listen alongsideMusic on the bones

Pyotr Leshchenko, “Serdtse”

“Heart” · tango by Isaak Dunayevsky · Columbia 78, catalogue DC 278 · the discography I read gives no recording date

In the 1950s and ’60s, Soviet bootleggers cut banned music into used X-ray film. Old X-rays, bought or picked out of hospital and clinic trash, were cut into seven-inch discs, the hole burned with a cigarette, and the result played five to ten times. Artemy Troitsky: “quality was awful, but the price was low, a ruble or a ruble and a half.”

Leshchenko, raised in Bessarabia and singing tango in Bucharest, was one of the banned, and “Serdtse” was his signature. He died in a Romanian prison hospital in 1954.

Poem · GreekCyrenaica, ca. 400

Hymn 6

Synesius of Cyrene (ca. 373–ca. 414) · excerpt, 16 of 37 lines · Greek from Migne, PG 66, col. 1609, via Early Church Texts · translation by Claude, unrhymed

He’s on your reading list. He studied under Hypatia in Alexandria and wrote her the earliest known reference to a hydrometer. When Ptolemais made him its bishop in 410, he hesitated, kept his wife, and stipulated his freedom to dissent on the soul’s creation, a literal resurrection, and the end of the world. The hymn opens with fifteen lines of Neoplatonist abstraction, cut here. I start where it reaches the stars.

Σὺ μὲν οὐρανοῦ σοφὰν ἄντυγα νωμᾷς,Τὰν δ’ ἄστρων ἀγέλαν ἀεὶ νομεύεις.

You steer the wise rim of the skyand forever shepherd the herd of stars.

· · · six lines cut: he rules the angels, and frees mortals from death · · ·

Ἱλήκοις ἐπὶ σῶν στέμμασιν ὕμνων,Βιοτᾶς ὑμνοπόλῳ νέμων γαλάναν,Εὐρίπων προχοὰν στῆσον ἀλῆτιν,Τερσαίνων ὀλοοὺς κλύδωνας ὕλας·

Be kind to these garlands, your hymns,and give the hymn-maker calm in his life.Stop the straits’ wandering outflow,drying up matter’s deadly surf.

Ψυχᾶς καὶ μελέων ἔρυκε νούσους.Παθέων οὐλομέναν κοίμισον ὁρμάν·Πλούτου καὶ πενίας ἄλαλκε κῆρας,Ἔργοις κυδαλίμαν ὀμφὰν ὀπάσσαις.

Hold off the sicknesses of soul and limb.Put the passions’ ruinous rush to sleep.Ward off the fates of wealth and of want,and give my works a voice of good report.

Ἐν λαοῖς ἀγαθὰν ἄνοιγε φάμαν·Πειθοῦς πραϋλόγου στέφ’ ἀώτῳ,Ἵνα μὴ νόος δρέποι σχολὰν ἀκύμων,Μηδ’ ἐν ταῖς χθονίαις στένων μερίμναις·

Open a good name for me among people;crown me with the flower of soft-spoken Persuasion,so my mind won’t reap a barren leisureor groan among the cares of earth,

Ἀλλ’ ἐκ σῶν ὀχετῶν ὑψιφορήτωνὨδῖσιν σοφίας νόον κατάρδω.

but from your channels, carried high,I’ll water my mind with wisdom’s birth-pangs.

A market too big, and a law too old

Wisne v. NCAA

CourtTenth Circuit, No. 26-1309 · Nov. 6, 2026 · published
HoldingPreliminary injunction reversed and vacated. The players’ market, “all of NCAA Division I athletics in the United States,” is implausible.
VoteUnanimous panel. McHugh wrote, with Kelly and Moritz. No separate opinions.

In June the NCAA moved from four seasons in five years to five in five, but not for athletes who had already used up four. A Colorado court ordered a fifth season for athletes who started in 2022–23. The Tenth Circuit stopped at the first step of the rule of reason: who competes for whose labor? A footballer can’t sell to the 101 Division I schools without football, and a gymnast can’t sell to the 303 without women’s gymnastics:

A men’s basketball player looking for a Division I roster spot, for example, does not explore what he might earn by selling his labor to a men’s hockey team.

A sport-by-sport “submarket” would need its own proof and data, and the players never offered one. The court expressly didn’t reach the NCAA’s argument that antitrust law doesn’t apply to eligibility rules at all.

Knife Rights, Inc. v. Blanche

CourtFifth Circuit, No. 25-10754 · Oct. 21, 2026 · published
HoldingThe 1958 Federal Switchblade Act’s ban on interstate trade in switchblades, § 1242, violates the Second Amendment. The facial challenge to § 1243 (territories, Indian country, federal enclaves) fails.
VoteUnanimous panel. Chief Judge Elrod wrote, with Willett and Wilson. No separate opinions.

Two and a half weeks old, and new here. It opens with James Dean’s knife fight at the Griffith Observatory in Rebel Without a Cause and the Senate’s 1950s juvenile-delinquency panic. Switchblades are “arms,” the court says; in the 1700s, button-release blades were fitted to flintlock pistols in case they misfired. The government showed a tradition of regulating how concealable weapons were carried, but not of banning their manufacture and sale. Of nearly two hundred pre-1900 blade statutes, two were close:

Two laws out of nearly two hundred bring us into outlier territory.

Painting39 × 51 cm

Diptych with Twelve Apostles & St. Paul

Workshop or circle of Wäldä Maryam (Ethiopia, active mid- to late 1600s–early 1700s) · c. 1700 · tempera, wood, textile · Cleveland Museum of Art 2023.4 · CC0

Cleveland says it’s sized for an Ethiopian Orthodox church or an elite home, in the bold style of the royal court workshops of about 1600 to 1750. The painter set Christ’s apostles in his own country and dressed them in fashionable cloth imported from India. They stand alike to show they’re a group, but each face and haircut is individual. Captions in Ge’ez name every one.

It’s a recent purchase: an Italian collector had it until 2005, then a London dealer, and Cleveland bought it in 2023.

PhotographColorado River, 1871

Light and Shadow in Black Cañon, From Mirror Bar

Timothy H. O’Sullivan · 1871 · albumen silver print from glass negative · Wheeler Survey album, Metropolitan Museum 1986.1054.22 · public domain

Look for the boat. O’Sullivan made A Harvest of Death at Gettysburg in 1863. In 1871 he went west with Lt. George Wheeler’s survey, and his job was to photograph the land to attract settlers. On the Colorado, boats capsized and the party faced starvation. Of the 300 negatives he took, few survived the trip back east. This print is from the survey’s own War Department album.

AIWho may take, and who may decide

A Brazilian judge stops Meta from training on the news

On 3 November a São Paulo judge, Guilherme Madeira Dezem, granted three Brazilian press associations a preliminary injunction. Meta’s new business terms, due 20 November, made a free license to scrape news sites for AI training the price of staying on its platforms. The judge suspended that clause, with fines of R$1 million a day up to R$100 million. In AFP’s translation, the demand is “a manifest abuse of rights.” Gazeta do Povo reports that he held that breaking articles into tokens to train a model is reproduction of a protected work, not indexing. The order is provisional, and I found no response from Meta.

Geneva decides on killer robots in eight days

The Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons holds its five-yearly Review Conference from 16 to 20 November. It’s expected to endorse a “set of elements of an instrument” on lethal autonomous weapons, agreed by experts on 4 September, and decide what happens next. Human Rights Watch says the final text was “significantly watered down,” but counts 76 states now expressing support, 70 of them voting parties: “the strongest political momentum yet for a CCW treaty process.” This is your question about machines and authority at its sharpest: weapons that, in HRW’s words, “select and engage targets without human intervention.” One link to today: the Convention’s first protocol bans weapons whose fragments can’t be detected in the body by X-rays.
